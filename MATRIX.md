## Per-chain gotchas

### [Ethereum Mainnet](chains/ethereum/SUMMARY.md)

- P256VERIFY at 0x0100 landed on mainnet only at Osaka (Dec 2025) but has been on OP-Stack chains as RIP-7212 since well before. A contract that feature-detects it will behave differently across chains that are otherwise "Prague-equivalent".
- "Prague-equivalent" is ambiguous downstream: EIP-7702 and EIP-2537 shipped in the same fork, and forks frequently adopt one without the other. Always record the EIP set, never just the fork name. This is the reason the eips: map exists.
- BPO forks change only blob target/max. A chain claiming "Osaka-equivalent" may still have a completely different blob schedule, or no blob market at all.

### [BNB Smart Chain](chains/bnb/SUMMARY.md)

- BSC shipped Prague SEVEN WEEKS BEFORE Ethereum mainnet (2025-03-20 vs 2025-05-07) and Osaka nearly five months AFTER (2026-04-28 vs 2025-12-03). "Behind mainnet" is not a stable property of a chain — it flips fork to fork.
- BSC took Prague's EVM half (BLS12-381, EIP-7702, EIP-2935) and dropped its beacon half (4788, 6110, 7002, 7251) via an explicit IsInBSC() early return. A fork-name-only survey would record "Prague" and be wrong about four EIPs.
- Custom precompiles at 0x64-0x69 leave only 0x12-0x63 of headroom before mainnet grows into them. This is the placement risk flagged from the Ethereum baseline, realised.
- System contract bytecode is REPLACED BY THE CLIENT at fork boundaries (statedb.SetCode). Code at a fixed address can change with no transaction in any block. Indexers reconstructing contract history from transactions will miss it.
- 0x64 and 0x65 become always-erroring at Pasteur (activation_time 1787625000 = 2026-08-25) — the same tombstoning pattern as Avalanche's native-asset precompiles.
- Only chain here with EIP-7702 besides mainnet, and the only one with zero transaction-type divergence.

### [Polygon PoS](chains/polygon/SUMMARY.md)

- PIP-88 reprices cryptographic precompiles and cold storage access at mainnet addresses with unchanged semantics. Gas benchmarks, DoS models and proof-cost estimates calibrated on mainnet are wrong here by factors up to 22x (blake2F per round) — and nothing reverts to signal it.
- Two system contract collisions with BSC at 0x..1000 and 0x..1001. Worse, both chains call 0x..1000 "ValidatorContract" while the code and ABI differ.
- StateSyncTx claims 0x7f, the LAST legal EIP-2718 type byte, adjacent to OP Stack's 0x7e. The top of the type space is being consumed downward while mainnet grows upward from 0x04.
- Fork activation is by BLOCK NUMBER, not timestamp — a fourth mechanism across this dataset. Tools reading fork timestamps get nothing.
- Chicago pulls in Osaka-era precompile behaviour (EIP-7823/7883 MODEXP, EIP-7951 P256VERIFY) without Polygon ever activating Osaka. Same out-of-order adoption as Avalanche's Granite.

### [Avalanche C-Chain](chains/avalanche-c/SUMMARY.md)

- NO EIP-7702. The C-Chain is Cancun-equivalent, not Prague — tx type 0x04 does not exist and no BLS12-381 precompiles are available. Wallets and AA stacks that assume Prague on any major EVM chain break here.
- P256VERIFY at 0x0100 exists WITHOUT BLS12-381 at 0x0b-0x11, because it arrived via Avalanche's own Granite fork rather than Osaka. Inferring a chain's fork level from which precompiles are present fails in both directions on this chain.
- Three precompile addresses (0x0100..00/01/02) are tombstoned: they always revert. They are neither absent nor functional — calling them fails differently than calling an empty account, which returns success.
- Atomic import/export transactions are not RLP and have no EIP-2718 type byte. Any indexer that enumerates "all transactions in a block" via the EVM envelope silently misses all cross-chain value movement.
- Cancun header fields are present but pinned to constants. blobGasUsed == 0 is enforced, not conventional — contrast OP Stack, which repurposed the same field to mean something else entirely.

### [Avalanche subnet-evm (L1s / Subnets)](chains/avalanche-subnet/SUMMARY.md)

- subnet-evm is a TEMPLATE, not a chain. Its precompiles are opt-in per deployment via genesis config keys, so two subnet-evm chains can present entirely different EVMs. "Runs subnet-evm" tells you almost nothing about a chain's actual surface — the genesis config is the real specification.
- The EVM can be PERMISSIONED. ContractDeployerAllowList gates CREATE on tx.origin; TxAllowList gates transaction execution on the sender. Neither has a mainnet analogue, and both change whether an operation succeeds rather than what it costs.
- Deployer permission keys on tx.ORIGIN, not msg.sender — a factory contract's ability to deploy depends on who initiated the transaction, not who called it.
- ContractNativeMinter lets allowlisted accounts mint native coin arbitrarily. Along with OP Stack's depositTx Mint field, this is one of two mechanisms in the dataset that increase native supply outside block rewards.
- Like the C-Chain: no Prague, no EIP-7702, no BLS12-381 — but P256VERIFY at 0x0100 via Granite.

### [Kaia](chains/kaia/SUMMARY.md)

- A TRANSACTION CAN HAVE TWO SIGNERS AND THREE HASHES. Fee-delegated types carry `feePayer` plus `feePayerSignatures` alongside the sender's, both as LISTS of (v,r,s) because either party may be a multisig account. The two parties sign different digests (the payer's commits to its own address). Hash(), SenderTxHash() and EthTxHash() are three distinct values for one transaction. Anything modelling "one transaction, one sender, one signature, one hash" is wrong on roughly half of Kaia's type space.
- ecrecover(signature) != from. The sender is an explicit FIELD; the signature is validated against the AccountKey registered on-chain for that address. Keys are decoupled from addresses: weighted multisig, role-based keys (separate keys for transacting, key rotation and fee payment), or AccountKeyFail, which makes an account permanently unable to transact.
- ONE TxTypeAccountUpdate CAN CUT AN ACCOUNT OFF FROM ETHEREUM TOOLING FOREVER. Ethereum-format transactions — legacy AND 0x78-wrapped typed, including EIP-7702 — are only executable if the sender's AccountKey is still AccountKeyLegacy. After an update, MetaMask, ethers and viem can no longer move that account's funds, and the failure is a validation error, not a signature mismatch, so it looks like a node problem.
- THE CONSENSUS ENVELOPE IS NOT EIP-2718. A typed transaction is `rlp(uint16 type) || rlp(body)`, and an Ethereum typed transaction is re-wrapped as `0x78 || <ethType> || rlp(body)`. The eth_ namespace adds and strips the 0x78 so RPC looks standard; block bodies, p2p traffic and the kaia_ namespace do not. Type numbers 0x7801-0x7804 do not fit in a byte. 0x78 also collides with Arbitrum's deposit type.
- THERE IS NO BLOCK GAS LIMIT. block.gaslimit and eth_getBlockByNumber's gasLimit are both the compile-time constant 500000000. What actually bounds a transaction is a SECOND METER: computation cost, capped at 150M units per transaction from Cancun. A transaction can fail with plenty of gas left, and gas estimation does not see it coming.
- extraData OVER eth_ RPC IS ALWAYS EMPTY BY DESIGN. The real field holds the Istanbul BFT validator set and seals; the eth_ marshaller replaces it with 0x. Four other header fields (sha3Uncles, nonce, gasLimit, difficulty) are synthesised constants or renames. Do not treat a Kaia block header from the eth_ namespace as consensus data.
- PRE-ISTANBUL, KAIA'S PRECOMPILES COLLIDED WITH 0x09/0x0a/0x0b. vmLog, feePayer and validateSender sat exactly where BLAKE2F, KZG and BLS12_G1ADD would later go. Kaia moved ITS OWN to 0x03fd-0x03ff at Istanbul — the opposite resolution to Tron, which relocated the Ethereum semantics instead. The old map is still compiled in for historical replay, so a full-sync node executes both.
- THE PRIORITY FEE WAS DISCARDED FOR TWO YEARS. Before the Kaia fork (block 162900480) EffectiveGasPrice returns the base fee and ignores the tip entirely, even though the tip was charged against the sender's balance in the fee cap check. Only half the fee is burned even now.
- 0x18 AND 0x40 ARE TOMBSTONED TYPE BYTES. TxTypeAccountCreation and TxTypeBatch are named by the type enum and by String(), and neither can be decoded — NewTxInternalData has no live case for them. They are permanently consumed.

### [Sei](chains/sei/SUMMARY.md)

- A FAILED TRANSACTION EITHER BURNS YOUR NONCE AND GIVES YOU A RECEIPT, OR DOES NEITHER. This is the answer to the question this dataset asks of every chain, and Sei's answer is unusually tidy. The EVM ante handler arms a DeliverTxCallback that bumps the sender's nonce even if the ante handler then fails — but ONLY when the transaction's nonce equalled the account nonce on entry. EndBlock then writes a stub receipt for an ante-failed transaction IF AND ONLY IF that same nonce bump happened. So an insufficient-funds or below-minimum-fee transaction at the right nonce burns the nonce AND appears in eth_getTransactionReceipt; a wrong-nonce transaction burns nothing and never gets a receipt at all. Sei does NOT reproduce Artela's trap of a spent nonce with no receipt.
- THE STUB RECEIPT IS NOT AN ETHEREUM RECEIPT. It carries txHash, transactionIndex, blockNumber and vmError (a Cosmos ABCI error string, not an EVM revert reason) and NOTHING ELSE: gasUsed = 0, effectiveGasPrice = 0, no logs, no cumulativeGasUsed, no contractAddress, no from/to. Zero gasUsed with zero effectiveGasPrice is the chain's own discriminator for "ordered, charged, but never reached the VM", and Sei ships a whole parallel RPC namespace (sei_get*ExcludeTraceFail) whose entire job is to hide these transactions from callers that cannot cope with them. A tool that reads gasUsed == 0 as "free" or status as "success" is wrong on every one of them.
- AN EVM ACCOUNT ON SEI HAS TWO INDEPENDENT NONCES. The EVM nonce lives in x/evm state under NonceKeyPrefix keyed by the 20-byte EVM address; the Cosmos account sequence lives in the auth module and is bumped by the SDK signature decorator. EVM transactions are routed to a completely separate ante chain (EVMRouterDecorator) that never touches the auth sequence, and Cosmos transactions never touch the EVM nonce. For an associated account the two numbers drift apart permanently and nothing reconciles them. eth_* sees only the first; a Cosmos SDK client sees only the second.
- OCC IS REAL, IS ON BY DEFAULT, AND CHANGES NOTHING YOU CAN OBSERVE. Sei runs a Block-STM scheduler over a multi-version store: transactions execute in parallel, a transaction that read a key an earlier transaction later wrote is re-executed, and after 10 whole-block passes the scheduler gives up and finishes sequentially. Conflict resolution is node-local; the invariant the repo tests is that the parallel result equals the sequential result exactly, down to events and per-transaction result codes. No gas is charged for a discarded incarnation, nothing about the parallelism is visible to a contract, and the dual address space plays no special role.
- THE PARALLELISM THAT *IS* CONSENSUS-VISIBLE IS THE ONE NOBODY ADVERTISES. v6.6 ships a second EVM execution path ("Giga", optionally evmone-backed), enabled by default, and switching it on calls tmtypes.SkipLastResultsHashValidation.Store(true) — the node stops checking a proposed block's LastResultsHash against its own per-transaction results. The in-source reason is verbatim: "since it may produce different gas used values." Sei's response to two executors disagreeing about gas used was to remove the consensus check, not to reconcile them. Only the app hash still binds; the per-transaction results header field is no longer cross-validated by anyone.
- A TRANSACTION THAT FAILS EXECUTION GETS A SECOND CHANCE, AND ONLY A SECOND. sei-tendermint's mempool caches successfully-executed transactions as permanently invalid but keeps a once-failed transaction eligible for re-execution ("Failed txs are given a second chance"); a second failure moves it into the reject cache. The same transaction hash can therefore be ordered into two different blocks at two different heights.
- THE BLOCK PROPOSER, NOT THE APPLICATION, DECIDES WHAT IS IN A BLOCK. Sei's ABCI has no PrepareProposal at all — the interface is a Tendermint v0.35-era one (ABCI 0.17.0) with FinalizeBlock and ProcessProposal, plus three non-standard methods the consensus engine uses to read EVM state directly: GetTxPriorityHint, EvmNonce and EvmBalance. Ordering, nonce-gap parking and fee-based replacement all happen inside the consensus engine's mempool, which is node-local admission policy, not a consensus rule.
- 0x0100 IS EMPTY AND SEI HAS P256VERIFY. It is at 0x1011, behind an ABI method selector `verify(bytes)`, priced at 300 gas per input byte, and it refuses DELEGATECALL. A passkey/WebAuthn wallet ported to Sei that calls 0x0100 with the standard 160-byte payload gets empty output — which EIP-7951 defines as "signature invalid" — so it fails closed, silently, forever. Confirmed live with a known-valid signature at block 226969278.
- EVERY SEI PRECOMPILE IS ABI-DISPATCHED. Raw-input calls revert. This is a category difference from every mainnet precompile and from every other row in this dataset: you cannot probe a Sei precompile by sending it bytes, and any generic precompile fuzzer or shim will see reverts rather than results.
- ONE ACCOUNT, TWO ADDRESSES, AND THE EVM ONE CAN CHANGE. A Cosmos account that has never associated resolves to `common.BytesToAddress(seiAddress)` — a raw byte-cast of its bech32 payload, unrelated to its public key. After it associates, it resolves to the real secp256k1-derived EVM address. So msg.sender and tx.origin for the SAME Cosmos account differ before and after association. Native balances are migrated across at association time (MigrateBalance), but ERC-20 balances, allowances and any contract storage keyed by the old cast address are NOT — they are stranded at an address nobody controls. There is no event a contract can react to.
- A BANK SEND TO A CAST ADDRESS CAN BE REJECTED. CanAddressReceive returns false for an address that is the byte-cast of an EVM address which has already been associated with a different sei address. The same 20 bytes are a valid recipient before association and an invalid one after.
- PREVRANDAO IS keccak256(BLOCK TIMESTAMP). Predictable by anyone. Contracts ported from mainnet that use it for randomness are exploitable and give no outward sign of being broken.
- THE BASE FEE IS NOT BURNED. It is paid to the fee collector. Sei's fee market is also not EIP-1559: ±1.89%/-0.39% per block toward a 250k-gas target, clamped to a governance floor and ceiling.
- BLOB TRANSACTIONS ARE ACCEPTED BY THE CODEC AND REJECTED BY THE ANTE HANDLER. Type 0x03 is listed in AllowedTxTypes and then unconditionally refused. The KZG precompile at 0x0a still works.
- RECEIPTS WITH type = 0xffffffff EXIST. Cosmos and CosmWasm transactions that emit EVM-shaped logs get a synthetic receipt whose type is math.MaxUint32, outside the legal EIP-2718 range entirely. eth_getLogs returns their logs. An indexer storing receipt type in a uint8 corrupts them.
- THE ETHEREUM BLOCK IS A FICTION AND stateRoot IS THE WORST FIELD IN IT. It carries the Tendermint app hash — a commitment to the whole Cosmos multistore — under the name and shape of an Ethereum state root. No Ethereum state proof against it can ever verify. mixHash is likewise the zero hash rather than the value PREVRANDAO returned.
- ADDRESS ASSOCIATION IS ITSELF A FREE, NONCE-LESS, TYPE-LESS TRANSACTION. The AssociateTx carries only a signature; its nonce, gas, value and recipient accessors all panic. Nothing in the Ethereum tooling stack can build, sign, decode, simulate or price one.

### [Gnosis Chain](chains/gnosis/SUMMARY.md)

- THE BASE FEE IS NOT BURNED. It is credited in full to 0x6BBe78ee9e474842Dbd4AB4987b3CeFE88426A92, and from Pectra so is the blob base fee. The 1559 parameters are mainnet's to the digit, so every fee formula ported from mainnet is correct and every SUPPLY model is wrong. Measured: the collector's balance rose by exactly baseFeePerGas * gasUsed over the pinned block.
- Withdrawals credit ZERO native token. The withdrawals array in a Gnosis header is an argument list for a system call to a GNO contract, not a set of balance credits — the recipient's xDAI balance is unchanged across the block. And the amounts are in mGNO: divide by 32 to get GNO. A withdrawals indexer written against mainnet is wrong twice over, in kind and by a factor of 32.
- Mainnet's deposit contract address 0x00000000219ab540356cBB839Cbe05303d7705Fa is an EMPTY ACCOUNT on Gnosis. Gnosis's is 0x0B98057eA310F4d31F2a452B414647007d1645d9. An empty account answers every call with success and no data, so staking code that hardcodes the canonical address fails silently rather than reverting. The other four mainnet system contracts (4788, 2935, 7002, 7251) ARE at their canonical addresses with byte-identical code.
- Pre-merge Gnosis headers are the same LENGTH as mainnet's and a different SHAPE. Items 14 and 15 are auRaStep (4 bytes) and auRaSignature (65 bytes) where mainnet has mixHash (32) and nonce (8). Clients tell the layouts apart by peeking at the next item's length. A positional decoder produces a wrong block hash rather than an error, and the JSON-RPC hides it by reporting mixHash and nonce as zero. Every block below ~40,000,000 is affected.
- Gnosis has twice replaced the bytecode of a live account by hard fork — GIP-31 at block 21735000 and Balancer at timestamp 1766419900, the latter overwriting an EOA's code specifically so EIP-3607 would stop its owner from ever transacting again. Both are chainspec entries implemented by both clients, and the live code matches the pinned spec byte for byte. Any assumption that "code at an address is immutable unless SELFDESTRUCTed or CREATE2-redeployed" is false here.
- The blob market exists and is priced a billion times higher at the floor: 1 gwei minimum blob gas price against mainnet's 1 wei, target 1 / max 2 blobs, and NO BPO fork has ever run, so Gnosis is still on Cancun's schedule while mainnet is at target 14 / max 21. excessBlobGas has been 0 in every block sampled — the blob fee on Gnosis is a constant, not a market.
- The block gas limit is 17,000,000 while EIP-7825 caps one transaction at 16,777,216 gas. A single transaction may legally consume 98.7% of a Gnosis block.
- EIP-170 arrived at Shapella in 2023, not at Spurious Dragon. Gnosis state may contain contracts larger than 24576 bytes, which no mainnet block could ever have produced.
- Fork lag has no sign. Gnosis was 110 days late to Shanghai, 1.8 days EARLY to Cancun, 6.8 days EARLY to Prague, and 132 days late to Osaka. Two of its four post-merge upgrades shipped before Ethereum's.

### [Sonic](chains/sonic/SUMMARY.md)

- AN UNDERFUNDED TRANSACTION BURNS ITS NONCE INSTEAD OF BOUNCING. The balance precheck drops `msg.Value`, so a transaction only has to afford its FEE to be admitted. It is then charged, its nonce is incremented, and it fails inside execution with a `status: 0` receipt. On mainnet that transaction is never included and the sender resubmits at the same nonce for free; here the nonce is spent and the sender must top the account up before sending nonce+1. - >- YOU ARE CHARGED 10% OF THE GAS YOU DID NOT USE. `chargeExcessGas` takes a tenth of the remaining gas before the refund on every transaction from a non-zero sender. A 21000-gas transfer sent with a 31500 limit costs 22050; sent with a 23000 limit it costs 21200. Both verified on live mainnet transactions. `eth_estimateGas` reports the true consumption, so the standard practice of padding an estimate is a direct transfer of value. Nothing reverts and the receipt shows the inflated figure as gasUsed. - >- THE BLOCK HEADER IS A 19-FIELD RLP LIST. No `parentBeaconBlockRoot` (mandatory on mainnet since Cancun) and no `requestsHash` (mandatory since Prague), while the EVM is at Prague. Verified by reconstructing the live block hash. Any header verifier or light client written against a mainnet fork fails on the field count. - >- `extraData` IS NOT VANITY DATA. Twelve bytes: the nanosecond part of the timestamp and the block's duration in nanoseconds. The duration feeds the next block's base fee, so discarding extraData discards a consensus input, and the fee market cannot be reproduced from the standard header fields alone. - >- `gasLimit` IS 5,000,000,000 AND MEANS NOTHING. It is a technical ceiling; real capacity is a per-second gas-power allocation shared among validators by stake. There is also no per-transaction gas cap today — geth applies EIP-7825's 16,777,216 only at Osaka, and Osaka is not active here. - >- PROTOCOL TRANSACTIONS HAVE NO TYPE BYTE AND NO SIGNATURE. They are legacy 0x00 transactions with `v = 0, r = 0`, and the SENDER IS READ OUT OF THE `s` FIELD. They sit in `transactionsRoot` next to user transactions; the only tell is a `from` of 0x0000…0000. Observed at block 78050318. - >- A PRECOMPILE CAN REWRITE ANY ACCOUNT'S CODE, BALANCE, STORAGE AND NONCE. EvmWriter at 0xd100ec00… exposes setBalance / setStorage / incNonce / copyCode / swapCode to the NodeDriver. Bytecode at an address is not immutable on this chain. The address also carries a one-byte `0x00` stub, so EXTCODESIZE says 1 and EXTCODEHASH says keccak(0x00) — it reads as a contract and behaves as a precompile. - >- 0x0100 RETURNS EMPTY FOR A VALID P-256 SIGNATURE. In the silent-P256 class (see findings.yaml:p256-silent-invalid), by a cause of its own: the feature is implemented and shipped in the running client, gated behind a fork nobody has scheduled. Indistinguishable from "signature invalid" under EIP-7951 — no revert, no error. - >- TYPE 0x03 IS ACCEPTED, BLOBS ARE NOT. A blob transaction with an empty blob-hash list is valid; one carrying blobs is rejected with `ErrNonEmptyBlobTx`. BLOBHASH always returns zero and BLOBBASEFEE is always 1. - >- FORKS ARE ON-CHAIN BOOLEANS WITH NO TIMESTAMP. A governed `updateNetworkRules` call flips a flag, effective next epoch, and the client records the block height. The ChainConfig handed to geth tooling has every fork time set to 0 and is documented as valid for exactly one block height. Call `eth_config`; do not read a fork schedule. - >- A ZERO-GAS-PRICE TRANSACTION MAY BE VALID. With GasSubsidies live, validity depends on what the SubsidiesRegistry contract returns during block processing, and the protocol may append a settlement transaction the user never sent. - >- TRANSACTIONS CAN BE SKIPPED. A transaction consensus ordered but that cannot execute in that order is dropped with no gas, no receipt and no state change, and processing continues. Inherited from Fantom and still live. - >- THE TRACER AND THE CHAIN RUN DIFFERENT EVMS. Consensus execution uses Tosca's LFVM (SFVM from Brio); `InterpreterForTracing` uses geth's. Two implementations, conformance-tested against each other, but a trace is not a replay of the same code. - >- A BLOCK-PRODUCTION SETTING CHANGES THE FEE RULE. Enabling SingleProposerBlockFormation clears `ChargeExcessGas`, so toggling an option documented as "can be enabled or disabled at any time" silently changes what every transaction on the chain costs.

### [Berachain](chains/berachain/SUMMARY.md)

- CometBFT DOES NOT ORDER THE TRANSACTIONS. The intuitive reading of a CometBFT-plus-EVM chain — consensus picks the transaction list in PrepareProposal, the execution layer runs what it is handed — is false here. BeaconKit's prepareProposal ignores the CometBFT mempool entirely and returns exactly two opaque blobs, the SSZ beacon block and the blob sidecars. EVM transactions are never CometBFT transactions. The list is chosen by bera-reth's payload builder over its own mempool, priority-fee greedy and single-threaded, and handed up through engine_getPayloadV4P11. Ordering and execution happen in the same pass, in the execution layer.
- A TRANSACTION THAT IS ORDERED BUT INVALID WHEN EXECUTED KILLS THE BLOCK, NOT ITSELF. There is no per-transaction skip at verification time: engine_newPayload is all-or-nothing, ProcessProposal answers PROCESS_PROPOSAL_STATUS_REJECT, and CometBFT re-proposes the height. An RPC client sees nothing at all — no receipt, no status-0 receipt, no orphan, no reorg, because single-slot finality means the rejected block never enters the canonical chain. beacon-kit's own test injects a transaction priced below the base fee and asserts exactly this. Note the contrast with the other CometBFT chain in this dataset: Artela ERASES the transaction (in the block, no index entry, receipt null forever); Berachain never produces the block. Same consensus engine, opposite failure mode — the difference is that Berachain's EVM is a separate process behind the Engine API, whose verdict is over the payload rather than over a transaction.
- THE ONE TIME IT DID HAPPEN, THE CHAIN STOPPED ORDERING. Prague3's blocked-address rule (Nov 2025) is a consensus rule keyed on EXECUTION OUTPUT — a block is invalid if any receipt in it logs one of eight addresses — which is exactly the predicate a block builder cannot evaluate while selecting transactions. bera-reth's answer was to gate its whole selection loop on `while !prague3_active` and log "Prague3 is active, building empty block". For nine days every block contained nothing but the mandatory PoL transaction. If you want a shipped, on-mainnet demonstration of why validity rules that depend on execution output are different in kind from rules that do not, this is it.
- NO PRECONFIRMATIONS, AND NO EARLY RECEIPT. Single-slot finality means an eth_getTransactionReceipt answer is already final and cannot change; there is no provisional block hash to look for. BRIP-0007 designs a sequencer that would serve 200ms partial-block state through overridden eth_* methods, but it is status Review, has no code in bera-reth v1.4.4, and the public RPC answers preconf_newPartialBlock with -32601. Anything built on that assumption today is building on an unshipped design.
- THE PoL TRANSACTION RUNS BEFORE EVERY USER TRANSACTION, and consensus enforces it in both directions: index 0 must be a PoL transaction and no other index may be. So no user transaction in a block can influence the distribution call. The coupling is across blocks instead — the inflation withdrawal at the end of block N is what the PoL call at the top of N+1 spends. And because the expected PoL transaction is derived from the header's BASE FEE and checked by hash, changing the base-fee floor changes the bytes of the block's mandatory transaction.
- "BERACHAIN V2" IS NOT A HARD FORK. The chain's protocol forks are genesis, prague, prague1-4, osaka and osaka1 — there is no v2 among them, in the client or in the genesis config. "V2" names product/tokenomics generations of Proof-of-Liquidity (PoL v2 / "PoL Next"), which ship as contract upgrades behind the PoL Distributor proxy plus two consensus-layer constants. The protocol surface it touches is already in this row: the Fulu inflation address and rate, and the fact that the Distributor is an upgradeable proxy the protocol calls every block. Do not go looking for a "v2" fork name in a client config.
- THE PREDICTION WAS WRONG. Berachain does not have a near-empty EVM delta. It raises EIP-170's contract size limit to 32 KB and EIP-3860's initcode limit to 64 KB, adds a transaction type, adds a 22nd header field, floors the base fee, slows the base-fee denominator sixfold, floors the blob base fee, and ships Osaka without PeerDAS. What IS empty is the precompile set: zero custom precompiles, zero divergences, stock revm.
- A 30 KB contract deploys here and nowhere else in this dataset. Size-limit tooling that hardcodes 24576 reports a false failure on Berachain; a contract developed on Berachain against the 32 KB limit cannot be deployed on mainnet.
- Type byte 0x7E is a COLLISION with OP Stack's deposit transaction, on a chain with no OP ancestry. Both are unsigned system-authored transactions from 0xfff..ffe; the field lists are different. Any decoder with a global type-byte map mis-parses one chain or the other.
- The first transaction of every block consumes ZERO block gas while running with a 30,000,000 gas limit and emitting logs (41 at the probed block). Receipt-sum gas accounting agrees with the header; execution-cost accounting does not.
- parentBeaconBlockRoot is a well-formed 32-byte root of the wrong object. The 4788 contract is byte-identical to mainnet's and the ring buffer is correct, but it commits to a BeaconKit BeaconBlockHeader whose body and state layouts are not Ethereum's. Restaking and validator-proof libraries verify against the wrong generalized indices and get no error, only a failed proof — or worse, a succeeding proof of a different field.
- EIP-7251 consolidations are accepted, charged for, queued, drained into requestsHash and then silently discarded by the consensus layer. Every layer a caller can observe reports success.
- Withdrawals are issuance, not staking. Every block carries exactly one withdrawal with index and validatorIndex both 2^64-1 — a synthetic entry the consensus layer requires to be first and which no validator owns. In 60 consecutive blocks there were no validator withdrawals at all.
- For nine days in November 2025 a block was invalid if any receipt in it logged an ERC-20 Transfer involving one of eight addresses. Transaction validity was a predicate over emitted logs, decided after execution. bera-reth's response was to build empty blocks for the whole window.
- Two execution clients, three fork generations apart. bera-geth's newest release has no osakaTime for chain 80094 and no Osaka1, so it cannot validate the current head; bera-reth is what actually runs. Pinning "the Berachain client" without checking which one gives an answer that is wrong about the code size limit, the base-fee floor and the blob schedule.
- The Engine API is forked: engine_newPayloadV4P11 / forkchoiceUpdatedV3P11 / getPayloadV4P11 carry a proposerPubkey argument, and engine_getPayloadV5 is implemented but deliberately unadvertised. Stock CL and stock EL cannot be mixed in either direction.

Flare adds ZERO precompile addresses to coreth's map. ### [MegaETH](chains/megaeth/SUMMARY.md)

- BLOCKHASH IS HONEST HERE. `BLOCKHASH(number-1)` executed at block 24869120 returned 0x42c631c2...3986, byte-for-byte the parentHash of that block and the hash of block 24869119. Mini-blocks are NOT addressable by BLOCKHASH, do not shift block numbers, and do not appear in the 2935 history. After Polygon zkEVM (returns a state root) and Monad (deferred execution), MegaETH is the case where the obvious suspicion is WRONG — the 10ms cadence is entirely outside the EVM's view.
- THE RECEIPT ARRIVES BEFORE THE BLOCK EXISTS, WITH A FAKE BLOCK HASH. A receipt delivered over the `miniBlocks` stream carries `blockNumber` = the EVM block that has not been sealed yet and `blockHash` = 0xffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffff — an all-ones sentinel. Observed: at the instant mini-block 0x91611854 was delivered claiming enclosing block 0x17b7a7a, `eth_blockNumber` still returned 0x17b7a79 and `eth_getTransactionReceipt` for that transaction returned null. The final receipt, 2.5s later, carried the real hash. Any consumer that treats the streamed receipt's blockHash as a block identifier will index garbage.
- Compare MONAD, and note that they fail in OPPOSITE directions. Monad's consensus commits an ordering BEFORE execution, so the block you see has no state root for its own transactions and the root lags by three heights. MegaETH executes FIRST and publishes the results before the block header exists, so the state you can query is ahead of the newest block hash. Monad shows you a block you cannot yet trust the state of; MegaETH shows you state that does not yet have a block. Both break the mainnet identity "a receipt names a block that exists".
- `block.timestamp` moves in 1-second steps while the chain produces ~100 blocks of activity per second. Anything needing real time must call the high-precision timestamp contract at 0x6342..0002 and accept a sequencer-attested clock — and pay the 20M compute-gas detention for reading it.
- Reading `block.timestamp` or `block.number` CHANGES YOUR GAS BUDGET. The 20M compute detention cap is invisible in the transaction, the header and the receipt, and the resulting failure is a halt, not a revert.
- Gas forwarding is 98/100, not 63/64. Contracts that compute forwarded gas explicitly behave differently here and nowhere else in this dataset.
- A LEGACY TRANSACTION CAN BE A PROTOCOL TRANSACTION. Type 0x00 from the registry's current system address to the Oracle pays no fee and is never signature-checked. Classification by type byte is not sufficient on this chain.
- Two coexisting height spaces: block.number was 24,869,417 while mini_block_number was 2,439,447,937. Only the first is visible to the EVM or to any standard RPC method.
- A MINI-BLOCK IS A RESULT, NOT A PLAN. It carries receipts with `status`, `gasUsed`, `cumulativeGasUsed` and the transaction's FINAL index in the enclosing EVM block, under a `receipt_root`. Execution has already happened and the transaction's position in the block is already fixed; the only thing missing is the block hash. Measured over 24 consecutive blocks and 577 transactions, the streamed order equalled the sealed order exactly, 24/24. Contrast Monad, where the committed thing IS the plan and the result arrives three heights later.
- A TRANSACTION CAN BE EXECUTED AND THEN DROPPED WITHOUT A RECEIPT — but only inside the block builder, and you will never see it. `commit_transaction_outcome` re-runs the block-limit check after execution, "to handle parallel execution race conditions", and a transaction that loses that race is erased: no receipt, no gas charged, no trace. Because the receipt is built after that check, the erased transaction never reaches the mini-block stream. The failure is real, tested in the tree, and structurally unobservable from outside.
- THE PRECONFIRMED RECEIPT UNDER-REPORTS THE FEE. A streamed mini-block receipt is field-identical to the final one except `blockHash` — and `l1Fee`, which is a constant 766,561,485 wei (0x2db0cccd) for every transaction regardless of calldata, against a per-transaction sealed value roughly twice as large. `l1GasPrice`, `l1GasUsed` and the scalars all match; only the derived total is a placeholder. An integrator settling on the 10ms receipt bills the wrong amount.
- THE SAME CHECK MEANS "SKIP THIS TX" TO A BUILDER AND "REJECT THIS BLOCK" TO A VALIDATOR. `BlockLimiter::pre_execution_check` returning an error makes the sequencer move to the next transaction; the same error inside the stateless validator, which replays a sealed block in order, becomes `BlockReplayFailed` and invalidates the whole block. Do not read a MegaETH limit error without asking which side produced it.
- `eth_call` ON MEGAETH'S OWN ENDPOINT DOES NOT APPLY GAS DETENTION. Measured with the chain's own MegaLimitControl contract: `remainingComputeGas()` returned 59,943,828 before a `TIMESTAMP` read and 59,943,792 after — a 36-gas difference, not the cap to 20,000,000 that the consensus rule requires. A ~29M-gas loop after a TIMESTAMP read simulated clean. The estimator the docs tell you to trust is blind to the one halt that has no mainnet analogue. (Its compute meter is also a fixed 60,000,000 budget that ignores the `gas` field of the request entirely.)
- Admission is synchronous and identical across all three submission methods — `eth_sendRawTransaction`, `eth_sendRawTransactionSync` and `realtime_sendRawTransaction` all return the same `-32003` for an underfunded sender, so the low-latency path is not a laxer path. A 50,000-gas transaction that mainnet would accept is refused outright: "Gas limit too low. Intrinsic: 60000".
- The RPC front end is not the Rust client. Submitting a transaction signed for chain id 1 returns "RLP decoding failed: Common chain ID chainId() { return BigInt(this._chainParams.chainId); } not matching the derived chain ID 1" — a JavaScript source fragment from an ethereumjs `Common` object, leaked into a JSON-RPC error. Error STRINGS on this endpoint come from a gateway, not from mega-reth, and should not be pattern-matched as if they did.

### [zkSync Era](chains/zksync-era/SUMMARY.md)

- eth_getCode(0x01) returns 608 bytes of EraVM bytecode while EXTCODESIZE(0x01) inside the EVM returns 0. The on-chain and off-chain views of the same address disagree by design, and the mask that produces this stops at 0xff — so at 0x0100 (P256VERIFY) both views report a real contract, where mainnet reports none.

- Transaction types 0xfe and 0xff are outside EIP-2718's legal 0x00-0x7f range, and both appear in RPC output. A decoder that treats a leading byte >= 0xc0 as legacy RLP misparses every L1->L2 transaction on the chain.

### [Cosmos EVM (evmd)](chains/cosmos-evm/SUMMARY.md)

- A FAILED TRANSACTION CAN BURN YOUR NONCE, TAKE YOUR FEE, AND LEAVE NO RECEIPT. The AnteHandler increments the sender's sequence and deducts the fee, and the SDK commits the ante writes before running the message. If the message then fails with a Cosmos-level error rather than an EVM revert — `failed to commit stateDB`, a refund gas-overflow guard, any recovered panic — the tx carries a non-zero ABCI code, and `TxSucessOrExpectedFailure` admits only code 0 or the literal log substring "out of gas in location: block gas meter; gasWanted:". Everything else is dropped by `EthMsgsFromCometBlock`: no receipt, not in `eth_getBlockByNumber`. The sender sees nothing happened, resubmits at the same nonce, and sits behind a gap they cannot observe. Artela shows the same combination reachable from ordinary bytecode; here the trigger is narrower, but the mechanism is the ancestor's and every Cosmos EVM descendant inherits it.

The nonce equality check was merged into `IncrementNonce` in the ante handler, so a stale or gapped nonce is an ante failure: the nonce is PRESERVED and no fee is taken (the ante cache is discarded), but the tx has a non-zero code and is filtered out of the eth view entirely. Safe, and completely silent.
- AN EIP-2718 ENVELOPE INSIDE SOMETHING WITH NO TYPE BYTE. The user signs an ordinary RLP transaction; consensus orders a protobuf Cosmos transaction that contains those bytes verbatim in MsgEthereumTx.raw. The Ethereum tx hash is the keccak of the inner bytes, the CometBFT tx hash is the sha256 of the outer wrapper, and they are different hashes for the same transaction. This inverts the non_evm_transactions category rather than fitting it, and it is a different phenomenon from Tron (protobuf all the way down, no RLP anywhere) and from Sei (the Ethereum fields re-encoded as protobuf, RLP bytes not preserved).
- EIP-2935 IS WRITTEN AT 8192 AND READ AT 8191. The preinstalled history contract is mainnet's, bytecode-identical, indexing at n % 8191. The keeper writes the ring buffer itself at height % HistoryServeWindow, default 8192, under a comment claiming that is "same as EIP-2935". BLOCKHASH is fine because it reads back through the same 8192; a CONTRACT that calls the history contract gets a real hash from the wrong height, with no revert.
- PREVRANDAO IS A CONSTANT. Not weak randomness, not predictable randomness — the literal value 0xffff…ff in every block of every Cosmos EVM chain, because BlockContext.Random is set to &common.MaxHash purely to signal post-merge opcode selection. Meanwhile mixHash in the RPC block is the zero hash, so the two places you would look disagree and neither is right.
- eth_getCode PROVES NOTHING ABOUT WHAT RUNS. Every ERC-20 token-pair precompile has a full, real, ~24KB compiled ERC-20 runtime stored at its address, and that bytecode is never executed — the native Go precompile intercepts first. The Flare and Sonic rows found chains writing a 1-byte stub to satisfy `extcodesize > 0`; this writes something that looks exactly like the contract it is impersonating. Only the source map, or the absence of code, is evidence.
- THE PRECOMPILE SET IS NOT ENUMERABLE AND NOT A PREDICATE EITHER. Base's dynamic range answers membership from the address bytes. Here membership is a lookup in x/erc20 module state, the addresses are sha256 hashes of IBC denominations, and the set GROWS ON ITS OWN — an inbound IBC packet for a new denom registers a new precompile and installs its bytecode with no transaction, no receipt and no EVM log.
- 0x0000…0803 IS ACTIVE AND EMPTY IN THE REFERENCE GENESIS. VestingPrecompileAddress is in AvailableStaticPrecompiles, evmd assigns that list to ActiveStaticPrecompiles, and no vesting precompile exists in the tree — so the availability check says yes and the map lookup says no, which is the branch that panics. Param validation checks syntax, duplicates and sort order and never cross-checks against the registered map.
- SEVEN PRECOMPILES WRITE STATE, INCLUDING TWO CALLED verify*. Staking bonds balances, distribution moves rewards, gov submits proposals, ICS-20 escrows value into an IBC packet, ICS-02 advances a light client. STATICCALL safety is decided per-precompile by an `IsTransaction` method, and ICS-02 classifies verifyMembership and verifyNonMembership as transactions — so they revert under STATICCALL despite being pure-sounding reads. Conversely the BANK precompile, the one everyone expects to move money, is query-only.
- THE GAS NUMBER CHANGES UNITS TWICE INSIDE ONE TRANSACTION. Cosmos gas at the ante layer is metered by a deliberately infinite meter; the EVM does the real metering; a stateful precompile then meters Cosmos STORE gas and charges the count straight back to the EVM as if the units matched. There is no conversion factor in the code because none is intended.
- GOVERNANCE IS A FORK MECHANISM. One MsgUpdateParams can change the active precompile set, reprice SSTORE/CREATE/CALL through the ExtraEIPs jump-table mutators, switch CREATE or CALL to permissioned or off entirely, move the EIP-2935 window, and rewrite the ChainConfig fork schedule. None of it requires a client release and none of it is visible as a fork in any Ethereum sense.
- THE "EIP" NUMBERS IN ExtraEIPs ARE NOT EIPs. The three shipped mutators are numbered 0000, 0001 and 0002 — the integers 0, 1 and 2 — and each deployment registers its own into the same global activator map. A chain that says "EIP 3 is enabled" is naming a private mutator, not a specification.
- TWO SECP256K1 ADDRESS DERIVATIONS SHARE ONE ADDRESS SPACE. Ethereum-style keys hash with keccak, Cosmos-style keys with ripemd160(sha256(·)). Both authorize Cosmos transactions and move EVM-visible balances; only the first can ever sign an Ethereum transaction, and `ecrecover(sig) == from` is simply false for the second. Nothing in the 20 bytes tells them apart. ed25519 is registered, charged gas, and then refused for every transaction.
- THE BECH32 ADDRESS IS NOT A SECOND ADDRESS. Unlike Sei, there is no association step and no byte-cast alias: the codec accepts hex or bech32 and returns the same 20 bytes. Every stranded-balance hazard in the Sei row is absent here — which is worth knowing precisely because the two chains look so similar from outside.

### [Injective](chains/injective/SUMMARY.md)

- A CHARGED, NONCE-BURNING TRANSACTION CAN HAVE NO RECEIPT WHILE STILL SITTING IN THE BLOCK. `CheckEthSenderNonce` bumps the sender's sequence in the ante handler and the SDK commits the ante writes before running the message. The `ethereumTxHash` event that the CometBFT indexer keys on is emitted by the MSG SERVER, after `ApplyTransaction` returns successfully — so any post-ante Cosmos error (`failed to commit stateDB`, the gas-overflow guards, a recovered panic) leaves the fee deducted and the nonce spent with `eth_getTransactionReceipt` returning null. An EVM revert is fine: it returns a response, not an error, and gets a `status: 0` receipt.
- INJECTIVE DOES NOT ERASE FAILED TRANSACTIONS FROM THE BLOCK, AND THAT IS A DIVERGENCE FROM ITS OWN FAMILY. `EthMsgsFromTendermintBlock` has NO result filter: every `MsgEthereumTx` in the CometBFT block is returned regardless of ABCI code. Cosmos EVM's `EthMsgsFromCometBlock` and Artela both gate on `TxSuccessOrExceedsBlockGasLimit` and drop failures out of `eth_getBlockByNumber` entirely. Injective ports that predicate but uses it only as a decode shortcut in `queryTendermintTxIndexer`. So the receiptless-but-charged transaction above is at least still visible by block scan — the nonce gap is observable here and is not on Artela.
- A WRONG-NONCE TRANSACTION PRESERVES THE NONCE AND PRODUCES NOTHING. The nonce equality check was merged into the ante-handler increment, so a stale or gapped nonce fails in ante: the cache is discarded, no fee is taken, the nonce is intact — and no `ethereum_tx` event was emitted, so there is no receipt to say so.
- THE EXCHANGE MOVES YOUR BALANCE BETWEEN BLOCKS. Injective's EndBlocker runs a frequent batch auction over the whole order book and settles DEFAULT-subaccount results straight into bank balances, which the EVM reads as `address.balance`. No EVM transaction, no receipt, no log, no trace — and usually no EVM transaction anywhere in the block. Any contract holding a balance invariant across a block boundary is wrong here.
- YOUR EVM BALANCE IS THE SPENDABLE BALANCE, SO IT GROWS ON A TIMER. GetBalance calls bankKeeper.SpendableCoin, which excludes vesting-locked coins. A vesting account's `address.balance` rises as the schedule unlocks, with no transaction of any kind and nothing to observe.
- THERE ARE NO GAS REFUNDS. RefundGas is disabled with a one-line `return nil`; the ante handler has already taken gasLimit x gasPrice. Every transaction pays for its full gas limit and every SSTORE-clear refund is discarded, while the receipt reports the real gasUsed. Over-estimating gas costs real money here in a way it does not on any other chain in this dataset.
- A 7702 TRANSACTION IS ACCEPTED AND DOES NOTHING. There is no accepted-type mask; a SetCodeTx passes ValidateBasic and signature verification, and then AsMessage builds the core.Message without SetCodeAuthorizations, so the delegation is dropped. No revert, no error. The ante handler charged intrinsic gas for the authorization list the executor never saw.
- P256VERIFY IS ABSENT AT 0x0100 AND FAILS SILENTLY. Proven live with geth's own VALID test vector, against a sha256 control on the same batch. Because EIP-7951 reports an invalid signature as empty output, every passkey verification here returns "invalid" forever with no error. In the silent-P256 class — see findings.yaml:p256-silent-invalid for the other rows and their causes.
- BASEFEE RETURNS 0 WHILE THE BLOCK REPORTS A BASE FEE. The EVM hard-codes BlockContext.BaseFee to zero and sets NoBaseFee; the JSON-RPC `baseFeePerGas` comes from the txfees mempool floor, a node-local admission price that is disabled by default. GASLIMIT likewise returns 0 under eth_call while the header reports 150,000,000 — so a contract simulates differently from how it executes.
- receiptsRoot IS A CONSTANT. FormatBlock hard-codes it to the empty trie root on every block, including blocks with real receipts. transactionsRoot is the CometBFT DataHash over ALL Cosmos transactions, not a trie over the EVM transactions listed beside it. No receipt or log inclusion proof can work.
- gasUsed COUNTS THE EXCHANGE. It is the CometBFT block's total gas. 54 of 60 consecutive probed blocks had zero EVM transactions and millions of gas used, and a receipt's cumulativeGasUsed (0xa75259) was two hundred times its own gasUsed (0xd715). Utilisation metrics computed from these fields measure Cosmos traffic.
- FOUR PRECOMPILES AT 0x64-0x67 — THE WORST ADDRESSES AVAILABLE. Exactly where Arbitrum puts ArbSys/ArbInfo/ArbAddressTable/ArbBLS and BSC puts its cross-chain precompiles, with opBNB already making two of them three-way. Injective makes all four four-way. None of the framework's 0x0100/0x0400/ 0x0800-0x0807 addresses exists here.
- A PRECOMPILE THAT TRADES, AND ONE THAT MINTS. 0x65 can place and cancel spot, derivative and binary-option orders, deposit, withdraw and transfer between subaccounts, dispatching each through the Cosmos authz keeper — so an EVM contract holding a grant can act for a user who signed nothing. 0x64 can mint, burn and transfer bank supply, unlike cosmos/evm's query-only bank precompile. Both are readonly-guarded, so STATICCALL is still safe.
- ed25519 CAN AUTHORIZE A TRANSACTION HERE AND NOTHING ON-CHAIN CAN VERIFY IT. cosmos/evm and Sei both refuse ed25519 at the same switch; Injective's copy returns nil. There is no Ed25519 verifier at any address on the chain, and the balances such a signature moves are the ones the EVM reads. `authorizes: protocol` with `precompile: none`.
- AN EIP-712 SIGNATURE SCOPED TO ETHEREUM MAINNET IS ACCEPTED ON INJECTIVE. The typed-data domain's chainId may be 1 as well as 1776, deliberately, so that MetaMask users need not switch networks. EIP-712's whole cross-chain defence is the domain separator, and this disarms it for chain id 1. The same path carries an optional FEE PAYER with a second signature — two signers on one transaction.
- AN ENVIRONMENT VARIABLE DISABLES SIGNATURE VERIFICATION. Setting DEVNET_NO_SIGNATURE_VERIFICATION to "true" or "1" replaces both the EIP-712 and the ordinary Cosmos signature decorators with a no-op, in the mainnet binary, read from os.Getenv at ante-handler construction with no chain-id guard. Clearly intended for devnets; nothing in the code confines it to one.
- UNPROTECTED (PRE-EIP-155) TRANSACTIONS ARE ALLOWED BY DEFAULT. DefaultAllowUnprotectedTxs is true.
- THE REPO MOVED ORGS. InjectiveLabs/injective-core is a 404; the source is at InjectiveFoundation/injective-core, while go.mod still replaces the SDK, CometBFT, ibc-go, wasmd and go-ethereum with InjectiveLabs forks.

### [Artela](chains/artela/SUMMARY.md)

- YOU CAN BE CHARGED, LOSE YOUR NONCE, AND SEE NO EVIDENCE OF EITHER — AND ORDINARY BYTECODE CAN DO IT TO YOU. The AnteHandler deducts the fee and does `SetSequence(nonce + 1)`, and the SDK commits those writes before the message runs. If execution then fails with anything other than an EVM revert — and executing PREVRANDAO is such a failure, because BlockContext.Random is nil and opRandom dereferences it — the transaction has a non-zero ABCI code, and `TxSuccessOrExceedsBlockGasLimit` erases it from `eth_getBlockByNumber`, from `eth_getTransactionByHash` and from `eth_getTransactionReceipt`. The sender sees a transaction that apparently never existed, resubmits at the same nonce, and is now permanently behind a one-slot gap that nothing but `eth_getTransactionCount` will ever reveal. The ante decorator that emits the index event says in a comment that such a transaction "shouldn't be ignored by json-rpc"; it is ignored anyway.
- A CONTRACT'S OWNER CAN INSTALL WASM THAT REVERTS OTHER PEOPLE'S TRANSACTIONS, AFTER THEY HAVE ALREADY SUCCEEDED. This is the row's headline. An Aspect is a WASM module bound to a contract; the protocol runs it at four points inside ordinary user transactions. PostContractCall runs after a call frame returned and, if it errors, EVM.Call overwrites both the error AND the return data and reverts to the frame snapshot. PostTxExecute runs only on the SUCCESS path of the whole transaction and, if it errors, overwrites vmErr — so ApplyTransaction never commits and the entire transaction's state is thrown away. Neither involves a REVERT opcode. The transaction that failed was not the Aspect owner's, and the gas was not the Aspect owner's either: join-point execution is metered out of the sender's remaining gas.
- THE PARTY WHO CAN BIND AN ASPECT IS DECIDED BY ASKING THE CONTRACT. BindHandler calls `isOwner(address)` on the target and, if that call errors, falls back to OpenZeppelin's `owner()`, comparing the answer to msg.sender. So it is the contract's CURRENT owner, not its deployer, and any already-deployed Ownable contract is bindable — including one whose ownership was transferred later. A contract whose `isOwner` returns true unconditionally can be bound by anyone. Nothing asks the contract's users, and nothing in the contract's bytecode or in `eth_getCode` records that an Aspect is attached; the only way to find out is to call `aspectsOf` on the AspectCore precompile.
- THERE ARE THREE WAYS A TRANSACTION CAN FAIL AND ONLY TWO OF THEM LOOK LIKE FAILURE. AnteHandler rejection during DeliverTx leaves the transaction in the CometBFT block with NO ETHEREUM RECEIPT AT ALL. An EVM revert gives status 0. An Aspect rejection also gives status 0, but with an error string that is not an EVM revert reason and a trace containing no REVERT. A client reading only `receipt.status` cannot tell the last two apart, and a client that trusts `eth_getTransactionReceipt` cannot see the first at all.
- A TRANSACTION IN A BLOCK CAN HAVE NO RECEIPT, NO `eth_getTransactionByHash` ENTRY, AND NOT APPEAR IN THE BLOCK'S TRANSACTION LIST. The `ethereum_tx` event that carries `ethereumTxHash` is emitted by the LAST ante decorator, so any earlier ante failure — stale nonce, insufficient balance, gas price below the now-current minimum, bad signature — leaves nothing for the CometBFT indexer to find. GetTxByEthHash then errors and GetTransactionReceipt returns null. EthMsgsFromCosmosBlock independently filters the block by TxSuccessOrExceedsBlockGasLimit, so the transaction is missing from `eth_getBlockByNumber` too. THE ONE EXCEPTION IS EXACT AND FRAGILE: a transaction that exceeded the BLOCK gas limit is kept, and it is recognised by a SUBSTRING MATCH on the Cosmos log against the literal "out of gas in location: block gas meter; gasWanted:".
- EXECUTING PREVRANDAO PANICS THE TRANSACTION OUT OF EXISTENCE. BlockContext.Random is `nil, // not supported`, opRandom calls `.Bytes()` on it, and common.Hash.Bytes has a value receiver — so 0x44 is a nil dereference. The Go panic is caught by the Cosmos SDK's runTx recovery, the resulting log is a Go runtime message rather than the block-gas sentinel, and so the transaction takes the erasure path above: fees deducted and nonce incremented by the ante handler, and nothing at all visible over JSON-RPC. 0x44 is also DIFFICULTY, so pre-merge bytecode hits the same trap. Compare Cosmos EVM, where PREVRANDAO is the constant 0xffff…ff, and Sei, where it is a function of the timestamp.
- THE CHAIN REPORTS CANCUN AND IS NOT CANCUN. Artela's own Cancun jump table enables EIP-1153 and EIP-5656 and comments out EIP-4844, EIP-7516 and EIP-6780 under a FIXME. So TSTORE and MCOPY work; BLOBHASH and BLOBBASEFEE are UNDEFINED opcodes that burn the frame's gas; SELFDESTRUCT still really destroys; and there is no point-evaluation precompile at 0x0a because the precompile switch stops at Berlin. Four different wrong answers behind one fork name.
- SHANGHAI AND CANCUN ARE BLOCK NUMBERS HERE. The ChainConfig proto has `shanghai_block` and `cancun_block`, and EthereumConfig() manufactures the ShanghaiTime and CancunTime go-ethereum wants as either 0 or MaxUint64 by comparing the current HEIGHT against them. Ethereum made both of these timestamp forks; Artela un-made that decision. Prague and Osaka have no field at all and are not schedulable.
- EVERY TRANSACTION IS BILLED FOR AT LEAST HALF ITS GAS LIMIT. ApplyMessage ends with `gasUsed = max(gasLimit * MinGasMultiplier, actualGasUsed)`, default multiplier 0.5, and the receipt reports the floor rather than the measurement. Padding a gas limit — the standard defensive habit everywhere else — is a direct loss, and gas estimators that learn from receipts learn the padded number.
- A TRANSACTION WITH v = r = s = 0 CAN BE VALID. If the calldata starts with the four bytes 0xCAFECAFE followed by a four-byte keccak checksum, and the target has code, the sender is not recovered — it is RETURNED by a verifier Aspect bound to the target, and accepted if that same Aspect is also bound to the address it named. There is no new transaction type and no envelope change, so every Ethereum tool parses it as an ordinary transaction with a broken signature. `ecrecover(sig) == from` is not false here, it is undefined. Verification costs a flat, non-refundable 150,000 gas added to intrinsic gas.
- CALLING THE ASPECT SYSTEM CONTRACT FROM A CONTRACT IS A SILENT NO-OP. 0x…A27E14 is intercepted in the keeper by comparing `msg.To`, before any EVM frame is built, and it is NOT in the EVM's own precompile map. So it works as a transaction target and, from inside bytecode, it is an account with no code — which go-ethereum's CALL reports as success with empty output. A contract that tries to bind, unbind or query an Aspect gets a successful-looking call that did nothing.
- DEPLOYING AN ASPECT MINTS A CONTRACT ADDRESS THAT HAS NO CODE. The aspect id is `crypto.CreateAddress(from, nonce)` — the same derivation CREATE uses, out of the same address space — and the transaction receipt reports it in `contractAddress`. What lives there is WASM in a separate store, so `eth_getCode` at an address a receipt says was just created returns `0x`, forever.
- A BLOB TRANSACTION IS NOT REJECTED, IT IS DEMOTED. go-ethereum v1.12.0 decodes type 0x03, and Artela's NewTxDataFromTx has cases for only 0x01 and 0x02 with a `default:` that builds a LEGACY transaction — so the type byte, blob hashes and blob fee cap are discarded and the transaction is re-encoded as a 0x00 while MsgEthereumTx.Hash keeps the original 0x03 hash. Because MsgEthereumTx stores the transaction FIELD BY FIELD in a protobuf Any rather than keeping the raw envelope, the original bytes cannot be recovered to check.
- BLOCKHASH IS NOT LIMITED TO 256 BLOCKS. GetHashFn serves CometBFT header hashes for any height in the current chain epoch out of the keeper's own store, so a contract can read hashes arbitrarily far back. Mainnet contracts that rely on BLOCKHASH returning zero beyond 256 blocks — a common cheap "is this recent" test — silently get a real hash instead.
- NO MODULE PRECOMPILES AT ALL, WHICH BREAKS PORTS IN THE OPPOSITE DIRECTION. This is a Cosmos EVM chain with no bank, staking, gov, slashing, distribution or IBC precompiles and no bech32 helper — the 0x0100 / 0x0400 / 0x0800-0x0807 block that the cosmos-evm and Sei rows document is entirely empty here. A contract moved from another Cosmos EVM chain does not revert when it calls one; it succeeds with empty output, because those addresses are empty accounts.
- "ELASTIC EXECUTION" AND PARALLEL EXECUTION ARE NOT IN THE SHIPPED CLIENT. The pinned SDK fork's DeliverTx calls runTx once per transaction against a single deliverState; there is no scheduler, no optimistic execution, no conflict detection and no re-run path anywhere in the five repos. Likewise the Aspect block-level proposal handler exists and is COMMENTED OUT of app.go, so PrepareProposal and ProcessProposal are the stock Cosmos SDK defaults and no Aspect ever runs at the proposal phase.
- THE NETWORK IS NOT REACHABLE. Every RPC endpoint published for chain id 11820 and 11822 in both chainid.network and chainlist.org, plus artela.network, docs.artela.network and both explorers, returned Cloudflare origin errors or refused connections on 2026-08-27. Nothing in this row rests on a live probe, and every claim above is a claim about what the pinned client WOULD do.

### [Moonbeam](chains/moonbeam/SUMMARY.md)

- THE EVM CAN BE SWITCHED OFF, AND WAS OFF AT THE OBSERVED BLOCK. The runtime's `BaseCallFilter` is `MaintenanceMode`, whose maintenance variant returns false for `RuntimeCall::Ethereum(_)` and `RuntimeCall::EVM(_)`, and `validate_transaction` rejects filtered calls before they enter the pool. At block 16790908 the chain was in that state: no block sampled after 16672926 contains a transaction or a log (the 40 blocks ending at the pin, plus three separate 500-block eth_getLogs windows), the last EVM activity ends at a timestamp of 2026-08-01T00:00Z, and dispatches through the precompiles reproduce the maintenance filter's list exactly — Balances and ParachainStaking return `CallFiltered`, while ConvictionVoting, Preimage, AuthorMapping and the TreasuryCouncil reach their pallets and return pallet-specific errors. Under the normal filter Balances and ParachainStaking would both be permitted, so this is not ambiguity. `eth_call` still answers throughout, because it goes through the runtime API rather than through the extrinsic filter — the RPC looks completely healthy while nothing can be included.
- `eth_getCode` proves nothing about precompile-ness in EITHER direction here. Mainnet's own precompiles 0x01-0x09 return 5 bytes of code (0x60006000fd); genuine Moonbeam precompiles at 0x0b-0x11, 0x0100, 0x0816, 0x0818 and 0x0819 return `0x`; and the tombstoned addresses 0x0401, 0x0803, 0x080e, 0x080f return code AND revert. The code is written per-address by anyone calling `PrecompileRegistry.updateAccountCode`, so the set is historical accident. Use 0x0815's `isActivePrecompile` if you need an answer.
- A precompile CALL can bond stake, vote in governance, register block-author keys and send XCM messages to other chains. STATICCALL is safe — the `#[precompile::view]` modifier is checked before anything else — but DELEGATECALL is not merely unsafe, it is refused: every Moonbeam-band precompile reverts with "Cannot be called with DELEGATECALL or CALLCODE", while the Ethereum-band ones accept it. Several also refuse callers by CLASS: Batch (0x0808) and GMP (0x0816) revert with "Function not callable by smart contracts" for any contract caller.
- `eth_getTransactionCount` counts Substrate extrinsics too. pallet-evm is configured with `FrameSystemAccountProvider`, so the EVM nonce IS the frame_system account nonce; a staking extrinsic signed with the same key bumps the number your Ethereum wallet is about to reuse.
- A transaction in a block may have a signature that cannot be recovered. XCM-originated EVM calls are stored as type-0x02 transactions with r = s = 1 and a chain-global nonce; they look like ordinary user transactions over JSON-RPC.
- Fork detection has nothing to read. No fork names, no activation timestamps, no block numbers, no client fork gate — the EVM ruleset is a constant inside a WASM blob that governance replaces in state. `spec_version` is the only handle, and it is not exposed over the Ethereum RPC namespace.

### [Tron](chains/tron/SUMMARY.md)

- 0x03 is named RIPEMD160 and is not RIPEMD160. It returns SHA256(SHA256(data)[0:20]). Contracts ported from Ethereum that call 0x03 get silently wrong hashes — no revert, no error, just different bytes. The real RIPEMD160 is at 0x020003.
- 0x09 and 0x0a are occupied by batchValidateSign and validateMultiSign, colliding head-on with mainnet's BLAKE2F and KZG point evaluation. The genuine Ethereum implementations were relocated to a 0x02xxxx shadow range rather than the collisions being resolved.
- 16 custom opcodes at 0xd0-0xdf make staking, voting and reward withdrawal single-instruction operations. Any EVM tool with a fixed jump table decodes Tron bytecode incorrectly.
- No RLP, no EIP-2718, no signed-transaction envelope shared with Ethereum. 43 protobuf contract types, only 2 of which touch the EVM. Address encoding differs too (base58 with a 0x41 prefix).
- Energy and bandwidth are staked resources, not purchased gas. A sufficiently staked account pays nothing. Fee estimation, gas golfing and 1559 tooling have no meaning here.
- BASEFEE returns a governance parameter. It is present, it is not zero, and it is not a base fee — the most subtly misleading opcode in the dataset.

### [Monad](chains/monad/SUMMARY.md)

- gasUsed IS THE GAS LIMIT. compute_gas_refund returns 0 from MONAD_ONE on, so execute_final's `gas_used = gas_limit - refund` degenerates to gas_used == gas_limit for every transaction. Senders pay for gas they never spent; receipts, block totals and the base-fee input all report limits. Confirmed live on two unrelated transactions at block 96823552, both with gasUsed byte-identical to the submitted gas. This is the single most consequential "the EIP is present but does not behave" case in the dataset.
- The header's stateRoot is a genuine post-state root — but consensus commits the block's transaction ordering BEFORE anyone executes it. The executed header of block N is carried in block N+3's proposal and validated there (EXECUTION_DELAY = 3). "The block is final" and "the state root is agreed" are three blocks apart. An indexer that treats a committed block as having a settled state root is reading a value no quorum has voted on yet.
- requestsHash is the ZERO hash on every block, not keccak of the empty list. Any Prague header verifier recomputes 0xe3b0c442..b855 and fails.
- The first transaction in a block may be a protocol transaction that looks exactly like a user transaction: legacy type 0x00, real EIP-155 signature, gas 0, gasPrice 0, sender 0x6f49a8F6..DC9e, recipient 0x1000, moving MON. There is no type byte to filter on. It is inside transactionsRoot.
- Staking lives at 0x1000 as a PRECOMPILE with no bytecode, colliding with BSC's and Polygon's ValidatorContract system contracts at the same address. Three chains, staking logic at one address, and EXTCODESIZE distinguishes them.
- Memory is priced linearly (word_count/2) and capped at 8 MB across the whole call stack. Cheaper than mainnet up to the cap, impossible past it, and the failure is OutOfGas no matter how much gas you send.
- Cold account and cold storage access cost 4x mainnet from MONAD_SEVEN. Contract code is unchanged, opcodes are unchanged, gas is not.
- CREATE inside a 7702-delegated account is an error here and legal on mainnet.
- A transaction only needs to cover its FEE, not its value, to be included. It is charged, its nonce is burned, and it fails with insufficient balance inside execution — a state Ethereum's validity rules make unreachable.
- A transaction can revert because some OTHER account it touched fell below its 10 MON reserve. Failure depends on the balances of accounts the transaction merely paid.
- Contracts can be 128 KiB. Anything that assumes EIP-170's 24 KiB ceiling — verification pipelines, bytecode indexers, proxy tooling — is undersized by 5x.
- Two fork ladders that do not line up: monad_revision (timestamp-gated, in the C++ client) and MonadChainRevision (consensus-round-gated, in monad-bft). Mainnet skips MONAD_FOUR and MONAD_FIVE outright. "Which fork is Monad on" has two correct answers.

### [Hyperliquid (HyperEVM)](chains/hyperliquid/SUMMARY.md)

- stateRoot is the zero hash in every block, at every height sampled across the whole chain history. HyperEVM headers commit to no state. eth_getProof is not served either. There is no way to prove a HyperEVM state fact against a block header, so light clients and trust-minimised state bridges are structurally impossible — and the field is still present and well-formed, so tooling reads a plausible 32-byte value and never errors.
- 0x0100 IS EMPTY. This is the first row in the dataset with no P256VERIFY at 0x0100, directly falsifying "0x0100 is the one universal address" as a universal. Worse, it fails silently: EIP-7951 signals an invalid signature by returning empty output, which is precisely what an absent precompile returns. Every P256 verification on this chain reports "invalid signature" forever, with no revert and no error. Any passkey or WebAuthn wallet ported here is broken and looks like it is merely rejecting bad signatures.
- Two consecutive blocks can share a timestamp. Every large block carries the same timestamp as the small block before it — two of 119 transitions in the scanned window. Any elapsed-time computation across that boundary yields zero, and anything dividing by it reverts. The dual-block docs promise increasing block NUMBERS and say nothing about timestamps.
- CoreWriter cannot tell you it failed. The entire contract is a gas burn plus one log; HyperCore interprets that log asynchronously outside the EVM. sendRawAction has no return value and does not revert on a rejected action, so a contract that places an order or moves funds gets a successful call regardless of the outcome. The same is true of the native bridge at 0x2222, which is a receive-and-log contract. Every EVM-level guarantee stops at the log boundary.
- 0x0a is a tombstone made by a build flag. KZG point evaluation is present in the precompile dispatch but always returns "c-kzg feature is not enabled", even for a well-formed 192-byte input, while mainnet returns a semantic error for the same bytes. It fails rather than returning empty, which is what separates a tombstone from an absent address — and unlike BSC's and Avalanche's deliberate tombstones, this one looks like a compile-time omission.
- The public RPC silently ignores the block parameter on every state-reading method. eth_call, eth_getCode, eth_getBalance and eth_getTransactionCount all return LATEST state at any requested height, with no error: CoreWriter returns its full 544 bytes "at block 1", months before it existed. Only eth_getBlockByNumber honours the parameter. Any historical simulation, fork-testing setup or archive replay pointed at this endpoint is quietly reading the present. This also bounds what THIS row can claim — see live_probe.caveat.
- No custom opcodes and no custom transaction type. HyperEVM reaches an entire order book from inside the EVM using nothing but 21 read precompiles and two log-emitting system contracts. Compare Tron, which needed 16 opcodes at 0xd0-0xdf for less, and Arbitrum, Base, OP and Polygon, which each burned a type byte. The interesting divergence here is not in the instruction set at all — it is in the block schedule, the header, and the asynchronous boundary between the EVM and HyperCore.
- PREVRANDAO is present and always zero, as is COINBASE. Tron removed PREVRANDAO outright, which at least fails loudly; here it executes and returns a constant that any attacker knows in advance.

### [Hedera](chains/hedera/SUMMARY.md)

- THE `eth_*` API IS NOT THE STATE TRANSITION. Blocks, hashes, roots, `gasLimit`, `miner` and the `latest`/`safe`/`finalized`/`pending` tags are computed in TypeScript by a separate process (`hiero-json-rpc-relay`, versioned independently — the live endpoint reports `relay/0.78.4` while the network runs consensus software 0.76.2) reading a mirror node over HTTP. Nothing it returns is signed, voted on, or reproducible from consensus. Everything about ordering, fees, duplicates and execution IS consensus. Confusing the two is the root of most wrong beliefs about this chain.
- ORDERING IS FINAL BEFORE ANYTHING IS EXECUTED, AND A TRANSACTION THAT IS INVALID WHEN IT EXECUTES IS BILLED WITHOUT EXECUTING. Hashgraph assigns every transaction a consensus timestamp by virtual voting over gossip, with no reference to its content. Handling happens afterwards, sequentially, in that order. `DispatchProcessor.processDispatch` charges fees FIRST and only then decides whether to call the handler. A transaction that has expired, has a bad payer signature, or fails a due-diligence check charges THE SUBMITTING NODE the network fee and the payer nothing. A transaction that is a duplicate, or whose payer cannot cover the service fee, charges THE PAYER and never runs. This is a fate no other row in this dataset has: not erased (Conflux, IOTA EVM, Artela, Rollkit), not executed-and-failed (Taraxa, Monad), but billed-and-skipped.
- A TRANSACTION IS IDENTIFIED BY PAYER + VALID-START TIMESTAMP, NOT BY ITS CONTENTS. Two submissions are the same transaction iff they share a `TransactionID`; nothing about the body enters the key. Submitting the same signed transaction to several nodes — which Hedera's own SDKs encourage for reliability — makes duplicates the NORMAL case, and the payer pays the node and network fees for every duplicate that reaches consensus, with the service fee waived. THE DUPLICATE NEVER EXECUTES, HAS NO EFFECT, AND IS INVISIBLE OVER `eth_*`: the Ethereum hash is written into the record only by `EthereumTransactionHandler.handle`, which a duplicate never reaches, so `eth_getTransactionReceipt` returns null for that occurrence forever while the HBAR is gone. If the SAME node submits the duplicate, the node is charged instead and the payer is not.
- HBAR HAS EIGHT DECIMALS AND THE CONVERSION IS APPLIED INCONSISTENTLY. The signed transaction's `value` is divided by 10^10 with integer division and NO remainder check (`EthTxData.getAmount`), so any amount below 10^10 weibar silently becomes ZERO and the transaction still succeeds. Inside the EVM, `msg.value` and `BALANCE` are in TINYBARS (eight decimals) because `weiValue()` is `Wei.of(tinybarValue)`. Over JSON-RPC, `eth_getBalance` multiplies back to eighteen decimals. THE SAME ACCOUNT'S BALANCE DIFFERS BY 10^10 DEPENDING ON WHETHER YOU ASK THE EVM OR THE RPC, and neither answer carries a unit. A contract comparing `msg.value` to `1 ether` can never be satisfied.
- CALLING ANY ADDRESS FROM 0x01 TO 0x2EE THAT IS NOT A PRECOMPILE CONSUMES ALL FORWARDED GAS AND REPORTS SUCCESS. `Version038AddressChecks.isSystemAccount` covers entity numbers up to 750; `CustomMessageCallProcessor.handleNonExtantSystemAccount` calls `frame.clearGasRemaining()` and returns success with empty output. On mainnet the same call costs 100 gas. Sending value to such an address halts with `INVALID_CONTRACT_ID` instead of creating the account. This is also why an `eth_call` to 0x0100 returns empty output — it is the sink, not P256VERIFY.
- `stateRoot` IS A CONSTANT AND `transactionsRoot` IS THE BLOCK HASH. `BlockFactory` hardcodes `stateRoot` to the EMPTY Merkle Patricia trie root in every block since genesis, and sets `transactionsRoot` to `txArray.length === 0 ? emptyTrieRoot : blockHash`. Neither commits to anything. `receiptsRoot` is the one root actually computed, and for an empty block it is 32 zero bytes rather than the empty-trie root the same header uses two fields earlier.
- THE REAL BLOCK HASH IS 48 BYTES OF SHA-384, TRUNCATED TO 32 EVERYWHERE. Both the relay (`toHash32`) and the EVM (`ConversionUtils.ethHashFrom`) keep only the first 32 bytes. The negative result matters: BECAUSE BOTH TRUNCATE IDENTICALLY, `BLOCKHASH(n-1)` DOES EQUAL `parentHash`. Verified live — the `eth_*` hash at block 99380446 is a byte-exact prefix of the mirror node's 48-byte hash.
- `eth_getCode` AT 0x167 RETURNS `0xfe` AND THE RELAY INVENTED IT. `ContractService.getCode` short-circuits on the HTS address "before consulting nodes" and returns one INVALID opcode byte. No such byte is in state. The five other Hedera system contracts — 0x168, 0x169, 0x16a, 0x16b, 0x16c — return `0x` from the same call. Contract-detection by `eth_getCode != "0x"` is therefore wrong in both directions on this chain.
- AN ACCOUNT'S CODE DEPENDS ON THE CALLDATA USED TO CALL IT. `ProxyEvmAccount.getEvmCode` takes the function selector as a parameter and returns account-proxy bytecode only for `hbarAllowance`, `hbarApprove` and `setUnlimitedAutomaticAssociations`, and empty code otherwise. Separately, every HTS token has synthesised, address-dependent ERC-20/721 proxy bytecode generated on demand by `RedirectBytecodeUtils`. There is no Ethereum account state that behaves like either.
- `block.basefee` IS ZERO AND `block.gaslimit` IS THE TRANSACTION'S OWN GAS LIMIT. `HevmBlockValues.getBaseFee()` returns `Wei.ZERO` unconditionally while `eth_getBlockByNumber` reports 1.11e12 for the same block; `HevmBlockValues` is constructed with the transaction's gas limit, so `block.gaslimit` varies between transactions in one block. `COINBASE` returns entity 98 (0x…0062) while the block's `miner` field is the zero address.
- GAS IS PRICED IN DOLLARS, NOT BY DEMAND. `TinybarValues.topLevelTinybarGasPrice` converts a tinycent fee-schedule price at the network's HBAR/USD exchange rate, times a congestion multiplier. The HBAR gas price therefore moves when the HBAR PRICE moves. There is no auction: `eth_maxPriorityFeePerGas` returns 0 and paying more buys nothing. The rate itself is readable from EVM code at 0x168.
- ONE ETHEREUM TRANSACTION HAS TWO PAYERS. The HAPI payer ("relayer") that wrapped it in an `EthereumTransactionBody` and the EVM sender recovered from the inner signature are different accounts. `max_gas_allowance` lets the relayer subsidise the sender, and on an ABORTED Ethereum transaction `chargeGasForAbortedTransaction` charges the RELAYER the intrinsic gas. Two accounts are debited by one transaction and the receipt names one.
- A SUCCESSFUL ETHEREUM TRANSACTION IS REFUNDED ITS HAPI FEE AND A FAILED ONE IS NOT. `contracts.evm.ethTransaction.zeroHapiFees.enabled` defaults true, so success triggers `feeCharging.refund`. Failure keeps the node/network/service fee on top of the gas. The surcharge for failing does not appear in `gasUsed * effectiveGasPrice`.
- EVERY RECEIPT CLAIMS A `contractAddress`. `TransactionReceiptFactory.getContractAddressFromReceipt` returns `receiptResponse.address` for every non-token-creating call, so a plain CALL — including a REVERTED one — comes back with `contractAddress` set to the callee. Any tool that treats a non-null `contractAddress` as "this was a deployment" misreads every transaction on this chain. Receipts also carry BOTH a pre-Byzantium `root` (hardcoded to the empty-trie constant) AND a `status`, plus a non-standard `revertReason`.
- `eth_getBlockByNumber` SHOWS A MINORITY OF THE BLOCK AND INVENTS SOME OF WHAT IT SHOWS. Record file 99380446 contains 7 HAPI transactions; `eth_getBlockByNumber` reported 1. Meanwhile `blockWorker` FABRICATES legacy transactions for logs whose transaction is not an Ethereum transaction, with `from` = `to` = the log's contract address, `input = 0x0000000000000000`, `gasPrice = 0xfe` and a zero signature. They cannot be re-encoded to their own hash and they never existed.
- `finalized`, `safe`, `pending` AND `latest` ALL RETURN THE SAME BLOCK — and here that is HONEST for three of the four. Hashgraph is aBFT: a transaction is final when it gets its consensus timestamp, there are no reorgs, and nothing can be revisited, so `finalized` and `safe` legitimately equal `latest`. `pending` equalling `latest` is the one that misleads: there is no pending pool view. A null `eth_getTransactionReceipt` on this chain means "the mirror node has not ingested the record yet", never "gone forever" — the opposite of Conflux, IOTA EVM, Artela and Rollkit.
- ACCESS LISTS ARE ACCEPTED AND IGNORED. `EthTxData` parses type-0x01 and type-0x02 access lists and keeps them only for `equals`/`hashCode`; nothing in the execution module reads them. No warming, no discount, no surcharge, no error. Re-encoding one is not even implemented.
- 56 OF THE 59 HAPI TRANSACTION TYPES ARE INVISIBLE TO ETHEREUM TOOLING AND SOME OF THEM MOVE YOUR MONEY. `CryptoTransfer` moves HBAR that `BALANCE` reads. `CryptoUpdateAccount` can replace the key controlling an account. `TokenWipe` destroys a holder's token balance with no ERC-20 event. `ContractDeleteInstance` deletes a contract and sweeps its balance with no SELFDESTRUCT in any trace. `TokenAssociate` means a transfer to an unassociated account fails for a reason ERC-20 has no word for. An indexer built on `eth_*` sees none of it.
- A BLOCK IS A TWO-SECOND BUCKET OF CONSENSUS TIME, NOT A PRODUCED OBJECT. `BlockRecordManagerImpl.willOpenNewBlock` returns true when `floor(consensusSecond / 2)` increments while the node is already iterating an ordered round. No quorum votes on a block, no proposer builds one, and `block.number` counts buckets since genesis. Inside the EVM `TIMESTAMP` is that bucket's second, so two transactions the protocol ordered strictly apart by nanoseconds can read the same `block.timestamp`.

### [Conflux eSpace](chains/conflux/SUMMARY.md)

- `eth_getBlockByNumber(N).stateRoot` IS THE STATE ROOT OF EPOCH N-5. Conflux defers execution by DEFERRED_STATE_EPOCH_COUNT = 5 epochs and the eSpace RPC republishes the pivot header's `deferred_state_root` under Ethereum's `stateRoot` key. `receiptsRoot` is likewise the deferred receipts root, and covers Core Space as well. Same invariant break as Monad, opposite direction: Monad's header carries a true root that no quorum has voted on yet; Conflux's carries a voted-on root that belongs to a different block.
- `transactionsRoot` DOES NOT COMMIT TO THE TRANSACTIONS IN THE SAME RESPONSE. It is the pivot block's own transaction root, while the transaction LIST is assembled from every block in the epoch. Live at block 155332816: one transaction returned, `transactionsRoot` = keccak256("") = the empty root. For an empty epoch the RPC substitutes the empty-trie constant. No consumer can verify an eSpace block's transaction list against its own header.
- `eth_blockNumber` IS NOT THE TIP. `BlockId::Latest` maps to `EpochNumber::LatestState`, so it reports the last EXECUTED epoch — four to five behind the chain's actual height. `eth_getBlockByNumber` at the real tip returns null for a block that is mined, ordered and final-ish. `pending` maps to the same executed epoch, so it is not pending at all. `safe` maps to latestConfirmed (~46 epochs back) and `finalized` to the PoS-finalised epoch (~200-240 back).
- A TRANSACTION THAT IS ORDERED BUT INVALID AT EXECUTION IS ERASED. Conflux has a third receipt outcome, `TransactionStatus::Skipped`, distinct from Failure. It is produced by nonce-too-low (permanently dropped), nonce-too-high (repackable), gas price below the base-fee threshold AT THE PIVOT BLOCK, sender-does-not-exist, sender-has-code, and — for Core-Space transactions — an expired `epoch_height` window. The eSpace RPC then removes every trace: the phantom-block builder skips it ("we do not return non-executed transaction"), no transaction index is written, and `eth_getTransactionReceipt` returns null with the in-source comment "a skipped transaction is not available to clients if accessed by its hash". No gas is charged, the nonce is not bumped, no state changes. From outside it is indistinguishable from a transaction that was never submitted.
- THE SAME TRANSACTION CAN BE PACKED BY TWO MINERS INTO TWO DAG BLOCKS. Both blocks can be in the ordered set. The first occurrence executes; the second hits `check_nonce`, gets `TxDropError::OldNonce`, and is Skipped — so it is in the Core-Space block body, is not charged, is not in the eSpace block, has no receipt, and has no transaction index. Duplicate inclusion is normal and invisible.
- A TRANSACTION CAN LOSE ITS ENTIRE BALANCE TO FEES. The EVM's "balance must cover gas + value" precheck runs ONLY under the devnet-only `align_evm` flag. On mainnet an underfunded transaction is executed: `actual_gas_cost = min(gas_cost, balance)` takes everything the sender has, the nonce is bumped, `gasUsed` is reported as the full GAS LIMIT, and the receipt is `status: 0`. Mainnet's validity rules make this state unreachable.
- `blockhash(block.number)` RETURNS A NON-ZERO HASH, AND WHICH ONE DEPENDS ON WHERE YOU RUN IT. In a mined transaction it is the hash of the block the transaction is in (the pivot hash is written to state BEFORE the epoch executes). Under `eth_call` it is the hash of the block 65,536 epochs earlier — confirmed live: `BLOCKHASH(155333001)` returned the hash of block 155267465. On mainnet it is zero. Any commit-reveal or "unpredictable randomness" pattern built on that identity is broken here in a way testing against a local Ethereum node will never show.
- BLOCKHASH REACHES 65,536 BLOCKS BACK, NOT 256 (CIP-133), verified live at both ends of the range. `blockhash(n) == 0` does not mean "too old" for about 18 hours.
- `block.prevrandao` IS ALWAYS ZERO while the header reports a real PoW `difficulty` and a real `nonce`. eSpace exposes no randomness of any kind.
- SSTORE zero-to-non-zero COSTS 40,000 GAS AND CODE DEPOSIT COSTS 400 GAS PER BYTE — both exactly 2x mainnet, from a single `evm_gas_ratio = 2` multiplier that also doubles new-account transfer and EIP-7702 authorization costs. Both measured live by binary search over `eth_call` gas limits.
- CONTRACTS CAN BE 48 KiB (49,152 bytes), twice EIP-170, and the failure at 49,153 is `OutOfGas` rather than a distinct size error. Verified live at the exact boundary.
- THERE ARE TWO BASE FEES IN EVERY BLOCK, and the one `eth_getBlockByNumber` reports is not the inclusion threshold. CIP-137 burns only part of the base fee — 36.84% at the current DAO parameter, measured from a live receipt's non-standard `burntGasFee` key — and a transaction is valid if its gas price covers the BURNT part. At the pinned block `baseFeePerGas` was 20 gwei and the real minimum was about 7.37 gwei. The split is governance-controlled and can change without a fork.
- A TRANSACTION'S GAS LIMIT MUST BE AT LEAST 100 x ITS CALLDATA LENGTH (CIP-130) or the transaction is invalid. This is a validity rule, not a price. No Ethereum wallet computes a gas limit this way.
- ONLY DAG BLOCKS AT A HEIGHT DIVISIBLE BY 5 MAY CARRY eSPACE TRANSACTIONS (EVM_TRANSACTION_BLOCK_RATIO). eSpace capacity is a function of the Core-Space chain's shape.
- BLOCKS CONTAIN TRANSACTIONS NOBODY SIGNED. Cross-space calls from Core Space are surfaced as pairs of legacy (0x00) transactions with `gas: 0`, `gasPrice: 0` and a fabricated signature — the first `from` the ZERO ADDRESS with `nonce: 0` (so the zero address has unboundedly many nonce-0 transactions), the second from a mapped address `keccak256(coreAddress)[12..]` whose key nobody holds. They have hashes, receipts and log entries. A block can report `gasUsed: 0x0` and still contain two transactions — observed live at epoch 155332825.
- THE JSON `r` AND `s` OF A PHANTOM TRANSACTION ARE THE CHAIN ID, DELIBERATELY. The RPC substitutes `chain_id` whenever the real r or s is zero, because zero "is not valid in some ethereum tools". The `raw` field in the same object still encodes zeros. Re-deriving the hash or running ecrecover on the JSON fields gives an answer that contradicts the response.
- CONFLUX'S INTERNAL CONTRACTS ARE NOT VISIBLE FROM eSPACE AT ALL. `InternalContractMap::contract` returns None for any non-Native address, so 0x0888..0000-0007 and ..000a are ordinary empty accounts here: no code, empty output on call. Storage collateral and gas sponsorship — Conflux's two most distinctive economic mechanisms — are Core-Space only, asserted in one line (`assert_eq!(storage_cost, U256::zero())` for every Ethereum-space sender). Nothing an eSpace contract can do touches either.
- THE EIP-2935 HISTORY ADDRESS IS A TRAP. The client writes epoch hashes into 0x0000F908..2935 on every epoch — but only `if` that address has code, and it has none. Deploying any contract there would activate a client-side writer into its storage.
- CORE-SPACE TRANSACTIONS SHARE THE BLOCK BODY AND USE A `cfx`-PREFIXED ENVELOPE. Typed Core transactions begin with the ASCII bytes `cfx` (0x63 0x66 0x78) plus 0x01/0x02, so an EIP-2718 decoder reads them as an unknown type-0x63 transaction. Legacy Core transactions are an untyped nine-field RLP list carrying `storage_limit` and `epoch_height`.
- TWO FORK LADDERS IN TWO DIFFERENT UNITS. Some CIPs activate on DAG BLOCK NUMBER and some on EPOCH HEIGHT, and the two differ by a factor of ~2.42. "Which fork is Conflux on" needs both numbers to answer.

### [Taraxa](chains/taraxa/SUMMARY.md)

- THE RECEIPT IS MISSING `effectiveGasPrice` AND `type`. `toJson(const LocalisedTransactionReceipt&)` emits exactly blockNumber, blockHash, transactionIndex, transactionHash, from, to, status, gasUsed, cumulativeGasUsed, contractAddress, logsBloom and logs — and nothing else. A consumer computing what a transaction actually cost has to join the receipt back to the transaction to recover its gas price. `src: taraxa-node/libraries/core_libs/network/rpc/eth/Eth.cpp:cumulativeGasUsed`
- ORDERING COMMITS BEFORE EXECUTION, AND THE BLOCK THAT COMMITS IT CARRIES THE STATE OF FIVE PERIODS AGO. A PBFT block for period N contains: the previous PBFT block hash, the anchor DAG block hash, `order_hash` (sha3 of the RLP list of ordered DAG block hashes), and `final_chain_hash`. That last field is `blockHeader(N - delegationDelay)->hash` — with `delegation_delay: 0x5` on mainnet, THE HEADER OF PERIOD N-5. So the object the committee votes on commits to an ORDERING for period N and to the EXECUTED RESULT of period N-5, and to nothing about period N's own state. Deferred execution, like Monad — but Monad's lag is 3 heights and is a stated design; Taraxa's is 5 and falls out of the DPoS delegation delay, so the same constant that decides when a delegation becomes effective also decides how stale the state commitment is. Before Cornus the field was the STATE ROOT of N-5; Cornus changed it to the block HASH of N-5.
- THE SAME TRANSACTION IS ROUTINELY IN MANY DAG BLOCKS, AND DEDUPLICATION HAPPENS BEFORE EXECUTION, NOT DURING IT. Twenty proposers draw from their own mempools with no coordination — `packTrxs` just takes the top of the local pool — so duplicate inclusion is the normal case, not an edge case. When the anchor is fixed, `pushCertVotedPbftBlockIntoChain_` walks the ordered DAG blocks and builds the period's transaction list through `std::unordered_set<trx_hash_t> trx_set`, keeping only the FIRST occurrence. The duplicate is therefore never a candidate for execution: it is not skipped, not charged, not given a receipt, and does not appear twice in the transactions trie. THIS IS A THIRD ANSWER, DISTINCT FROM CONFLUX'S. Conflux executes the duplicate, hits `OldNonce`, marks it `TransactionStatus::Skipped` and then erases every trace of it; Taraxa never orders it in the first place. The observable difference: on Conflux the duplicate is in the Core-Space block body and invisible to eSpace, while on Taraxa the DAG block that contained the duplicate is fully present in the period data — you can see the duplicate in the DAG, just not in the executed list.
- DUPLICATE INCLUSION IS PAID FOR IN REWARDS, NOT IN SLASHING, AND THE RULE CHANGED AT ASPEN. Before Aspen part one, `processDagBlocks` credited a DAG block with a `dag_blocks_count_` share of the block reward ONLY IF it contributed at least one unique transaction: a block full of duplicates earned nothing at all. From Aspen part one, `processDagBlocksAspen` replaced that with a different test — the block reward goes to every DAG block whose VDF difficulty equals the MINIMUM difficulty among the period's DAG blocks — while transaction fees still go strictly to the first includer. So the penalty for losing a race moved from "you get nothing" to "you get the block share but no fees". Nobody is slashed and nobody is charged. Against Autonomys, where an invalid bundle slashes the operator automatically, Taraxa sits at the far mild end: the worst outcome of racing is forgone revenue.
- A TRANSACTION THAT IS ORDERED BUT INVALID WHEN IT EXECUTES IS INCLUDED, CHARGED, AND GIVEN A RECEIPT WITH status 0. There is no skip path. `EVM.Main` returns a `ConsensusErr` for nonce-too-low, insufficient balance for gas, intrinsic gas not covered, and insufficient balance for the top-level transfer, and `finalize_` turns `r.code_err.empty() && r.consensus_err.empty()` straight into the receipt's status bit. Every one of those transactions is in the block, in the transactions trie, in the receipts trie, and returned by `eth_getTransactionReceipt`. The gas charged is the FULL GAS LIMIT (`consensusErr(ret, gas_cap, err)`) for nonce-too-low, intrinsic-gas and transfer-underflow. This is the opposite pole from Conflux, which erases the same transaction entirely, and from Autonomys, which drops the whole bundle.
- AN UNDERFUNDED TRANSACTION IS EXECUTED AND TAKES ESSENTIALLY THE SENDER'S ENTIRE BALANCE. If the sender cannot cover `gas_limit * gas_price`, `EVM.Main` does not reject: it computes `available_funds_gas = balance / gas_price`, subtracts `available_funds_gas * gas_price` — everything but sub-gas-price dust — and reports `gasUsed = available_funds_gas` with a status-0 receipt. From Cornus it also bumps the nonce to `tx.nonce + 1`. Mainnet's validity rules make this state unreachable, because a transaction that cannot pay is never includable. Conflux does the same thing by a different route (`min(gas_cost, balance)`), so this is now a two-chain class: DAG chains where inclusion is decided before balances are known end up confiscating rather than rejecting.
- NONCE SKIPPING IS PERMANENT AND THE ACCOUNT NONCE JUMPS. The nonce-too-high check is present in the source but COMMENTED OUT, above the line "Nonce skipping is permanently enabled now. Uncomment this part to have strict nonce ordering". Only `tx.nonce < account.nonce` is rejected. On success the account nonce is set to `tx.nonce + 1`, NOT incremented — so a transaction with nonce 1,000,000 from an account at nonce 3 executes, and nonces 3 through 999,999 are burned forever. The mainnet invariant "an account's nonce equals the number of transactions it has sent" is false here; `eth_getTransactionCount` is a next-nonce oracle and nothing more. Contract addresses from CREATE derive from `tx.Nonce`, not from a sequential counter, so a deployer can choose its own contract address by choosing a nonce.
- THERE IS NO EARLY RECEIPT, BUT `eth_getTransactionByHash` IS BROKEN FOR EXACTLY THE TRANSACTIONS THAT WOULD NEED ONE. `get_transaction_receipt` looks up `transactionLocation` first and returns null when there is none, so a transaction sitting in a DAG block that has not yet been anchored has NO receipt — correct, and the opposite of MegaETH's 0xffff..ff sentinel. But `get_transaction` calls `get_trx(h)` FIRST, which reads the `transactions` column where DAG-included and pooled transactions live, and on a hit dereferences `*loc` and `*final_chain->blockHash(loc->period)` with NO null check. `transactionLocation` returns `std::nullopt` for anything not yet finalized. So the one query a user makes about a transaction that is in the DAG but not yet in a period — "where is my transaction?" — reads through a disengaged `std::optional`. This is a source reading; with no reachable RPC it could not be confirmed against a running node, and what a release build actually returns is unspecified.
- `block.number` COUNTS PBFT PERIODS. It is not the DAG block count, not the transaction count, and not a wall-clock tick: `setFromPbft` sets `number = pbft.getPeriod()` and `appendBlock` independently sets it to `last_block->number + 1`. A period that anchors 50 DAG blocks and 900 transactions is ONE block.number; a period whose PBFT proposer found no new DAG blocks proposes a NULL ANCHOR and still produces a block, with zero transactions and an unchanged state root. So empty blocks on Taraxa mean "the DAG produced nothing new", not "nobody transacted".
- BLOCKHASH IS HONEST — the negative result. `opBlockhash` is unmodified geth 1.8 (256-block window, `lower = upper - 256`) and its `get_hash` is `FinalChain::blockHash`, which returns the same synthesised header hash that `appendBlock` stored and that `eth_getBlockByNumber` reports as `hash` and `parentHash`. So `BLOCKHASH(number-1) == parentHash` holds. The DAG is entirely invisible to BLOCKHASH: DAG block hashes are not addressable, do not consume block numbers, and never appear in the 256-block window. After Polygon zkEVM (returns a state root) and Conflux, this is worth stating explicitly rather than assuming.
- A GAS ESTIMATE IS A BLOCK-VALIDITY RULE. A DAG block carries a `gas_estimation` field, and `DagManager::verifyBlock` rejects the block unless that field EXACTLY equals `trx_mgr_->estimateTransactions(block_txs, proposal_period)`. For any transaction whose gas limit is at most `kEstimateGasLimit` (200,000) the estimate is just its declared gas limit; above that, it is the `gas_used` of a SPECULATIVE EXECUTION of that transaction against the state at the proposal period. So the output of an `eth_call`-style dry run is consensus-critical: two implementations that disagree about how much gas a speculative execution consumes reject each other's DAG blocks. This is the strongest coupling between the estimation path and consensus in this dataset — most chains treat estimation as a convenience API with no consensus weight whatsoever.
- THE 2,300-GAS STIPEND CAN WRITE STORAGE. Repeated here because it is the finding most likely to cost someone money: Taraxa ships EIP-1283 without EIP-1706's sentry, so a `transfer()` recipient's fallback can SSTORE a dirty slot for 200 gas. Ethereum delayed Constantinople by an emergency fork to avoid exactly this. Every reentrancy audit that reasons "only 2,300 gas, so no state change" is invalid on this chain.
- THE FALCON-512 PRECOMPILE RETURNS ZERO FOR VALID. `0x...fa1c` returns `bytes32(0)` when the signature verifies and `bytes32(1)` when it does not, and it wants an ABI selector (0xde8f50a1) in front of its arguments. Both conventions are inverted or unprecedented relative to every other signature precompile on every chain in this dataset.
- THIS ROW HAS NO LIVE EVIDENCE. Every RPC endpoint the chain's documentation still publishes fails DNS resolution, and every third-party proxy advertising chain 841 answers `eth_chainId` from static config and fails everything else. That is the inverse of the Hyperliquid problem — full source, no reachable network — and it means nothing here has been checked against what validators run. Where the pinned tag and the live chain could differ, this row cannot tell you which is right.

### [Autonomys Auto EVM](chains/autonomys/SUMMARY.md)

- ORDERING COMMITS BEFORE EXECUTION, ON A DIFFERENT CHAIN. Auto EVM has no block producer. Consensus-chain farmers order operator-submitted BUNDLES of already-signed domain extrinsics without executing any of them; domain operators execute the ordered result afterwards and commit to it with an `ExecutionReceipt` attached to a later bundle. Fraud proofs, not re-execution by consensus, are the correctness mechanism.
- A SINGLE INVALID TRANSACTION DESTROYS ITS ENTIRE BUNDLE. This is the answer to "what happens to a transaction that is validly ordered but invalid when it executes", and it is not any of the usual answers. `batch_check_bundle_validity` classifies a bundle with `InvalidBundleType` — `UndecodableTx`, `OutOfRangeTx`, `IllegalTx` (the fee/nonce/balance case), `InvalidXDM`, `InherentExtrinsic`, `InvalidBundleWeight` — and a bundle so classified contributes ZERO extrinsics to the domain block. Not the offending transaction: the whole bundle. Every other transaction packed alongside it — valid, correctly signed, already ordered by consensus — is discarded with no receipt, no log, no trace and no block position. `eth_getTransactionReceipt` returns null forever for all of them. Compare Conflux, which erases the single offending transaction; here the blast radius is every co-passenger.
- AND IT IS A SLASHABLE OPERATOR FAULT, NOT A TRANSACTION FAILURE. When the domain block is confirmed, `pallet_domains` collects `invalid_bundle_authors` from the receipt's `InboxedBundle` entries and calls `do_mark_operators_as_slashed(.., SlashedReason::InvalidBundle(..))` — the enum variant's own doc comment reads "Operator produced bad bundle". Slashing is automatic on confirmation; no fraud proof is needed for it, because the receipt itself records which bundles were invalid. So on this chain "your transaction had a stale nonce" is not an outcome the transaction can have. It is a protocol violation attributed to the operator who accepted it, priced in staked AI3. No other row in this dataset makes an ordinary EVM validity failure a slashable offence.
- `eth_getBlockByNumber("finalized")` IS ABOUT A DAY BEHIND `"latest"`. Measured live: latest 4,580,360, finalized 4,565,366 — 14,994 blocks. That is `domain_block_pruning_depth = 14,400` domain blocks (the fraud-proof challenge window) plus the consensus chain's own confirmation depth. `"safe"` returns the same block as `"finalized"`. Any bridge, exchange or indexer that waits for `finalized` on this chain waits ~24 hours; any that treats `latest` as settled is inside an open dispute window.
- Twelve of mainnet's seventeen precompiles are missing, and missing means the CALL SUCCEEDS WITH EMPTY OUTPUT. The precompile set is 0x01-0x05 plus 0x0400, 0x0401, 0x0800. BN254 (0x06-0x08), BLAKE2F (0x09), KZG (0x0a), the entire BLS12-381 block (0x0b-0x11) and P256VERIFY (0x0100) are ordinary empty accounts. A Groth16 verifier's final pairing check returns zero-length data: implementations that read the returned word reject every valid proof; implementations that only check the `success` flag accept every proof.
- BLOCKHASH reaches back 2400 blocks, not 256. Probed to the boundary: n-2400 returns a hash, n-2401 returns zero. `blockhash(n) == 0` is not a test for "older than 256 blocks" here.
- BLOBHASH (0x49) and BLOBBASEFEE (0x4a) are INVALID OPCODES, not zero-returning ones, even though the runtime installs `Config::osaka()`. Bytecode containing either cannot execute.
- PREVRANDAO is always 0 and COINBASE is always the zero address, on every block, silently. `FindAuthorTruncated` returns `None` by construction and no randomness source is wired in.
- There is no EIP-1559 base fee. `baseFeePerGas` is a Substrate weight-fee conversion whose input is domain-block Weight fullness and part of whose value is set by the CONSENSUS chain's byte fee. Two consecutive empty blocks raised it. `eth_feeHistory`'s projected next-block base fee is computed by `fc-rpc` with the 1559 formula and is fiction.
- An account can be REAPED. Balances live in Substrate's `pallet-balances` under an existential deposit of 10^12 wei (10^-6 AI3); a balance that falls below it is deleted and the residue BURNED by `DustRemovalHandler`. Symmetrically, a transfer that would leave the RECIPIENT under the deposit is refused — so sending 1 wei to a fresh address fails here and succeeds on mainnet. The EVM's account model cannot express either rule.
- Cross-domain AI3 arrives with no transaction. XDM deposits execute as `pallet_messenger` Substrate extrinsics in the same domain block; the balance moves and `eth_getBlockByNumber` shows an empty transaction list. Reconstructing balances from `eth_*` transactions misses every inbound bridge transfer.
- The domain's whole ruleset can be replaced from the consensus chain, and so can the right to deploy a contract. `ContractCreationAllowedBy` is flipped by an inherent injected by the domain owner or root; when it is not `Anyone`, a CREATE from a non-allowlisted signer is rejected at admission with a custom invalid-transaction code — no receipt, no revert string. Auto EVM is currently a `Public` domain at the default, but the rule is one consensus-chain call away and leaves no EVM-visible trace.
- `eth_getProof` is not implemented (`Method not found`). Any light client, storage-proof bridge or account-proof verifier that needs it has no path here, and the Substrate trie underneath is not an Ethereum MPT anyway.
- `EXTCODESIZE(0x01) == 5`. Genesis writes a five-byte `0x60006000fd` revert stub at 0x01-0x05, 0x0400 and 0x0401 — but not at 0x0800, which is a live precompile with no code. `eth_getCode` classifies seven of eight correctly here and the eighth wrongly, in both directions from mainnet.

### [Plasma](chains/plasma/SUMMARY.md)

- ZERO-FEE USDT IS NOT A CONSENSUS FEATURE HERE. Every sampled transaction paid a fee, the only live fee token is XPL, and the execution client has no code that could implement a fee exemption. Anything that looks free to a user is an ERC-4337 paymaster reimbursing a bundler, at the application layer, on an unmodified EVM.
- Custom gas tokens and confidential payments are ROADMAP. Plasma's docs use the future tense for both; the mainnet fee-token table has one row.
- Blob transactions are accepted by the encoder and rejected by the pool: max blob count is 0. Tooling that assumes a Prague chain takes type-0x03 will fail at submission, not at signing.
- `finalized` trailed the head by ~2,662 blocks (~44 minutes) when probed, despite BFT consensus advertising deterministic finality. Whichever component sets the finalized tag over the Engine API is lagging far behind the consensus commit, and that component is closed source.
- The block `miner` is a Gnosis Safe from the genesis alloc, not a validator key. Attribution of blocks to validators is not possible from the header.
- The Ethereum beacon deposit contract is deployed at its mainnet address with real bytecode and storage, and the client is configured to treat it as THE deposit contract, on a chain that does not use beacon deposits for validator selection.
